# Answer Key & Rationales

_NCLEX-RN Practice Test_

---

**1.** B. Early signs of hypovolemic shock; notify the provider immediately

Rationale: A progressive rise in heart rate and respiratory rate together with a falling blood pressure over a short period is a classic early compensatory trend for hypovolemic shock, often from occult postoperative bleeding, and the nurse should notify the provider immediately rather than waiting for overt hypotension or further deterioration. This is not an expected anesthesia-related fluctuation (A); the temperature trend is not suggestive of infection at this early postoperative point (C), since postoperative infection typically develops later and would not explain this hemodynamic trend; and a rising heart rate with a falling blood pressure is not a sign of adequate pain control (D).

_Category: Reduction of Risk Potential — Vital Signs | Clinical judgment: Analyze Cues | Difficulty: moderate_

**2.** B. Tension pneumothorax

Rationale: Tracheal deviation away from the affected side, absent breath sounds, severe respiratory distress, jugular venous distention, and hypotension indicate a tension pneumothorax, in which trapped air under increasing pressure collapses the lung and shifts the mediastinum, compressing the great vessels and impairing venous return — a life-threatening emergency requiring immediate needle decompression. A simple pneumothorax does not cause mediastinal shift or hemodynamic instability. Flail chest presents with paradoxical chest wall movement rather than tracheal deviation. Pulmonary contusion causes hypoxia and dyspnea without tracheal shift or unilateral absent breath sounds.

_Category: Physiological Adaptation — Medical Emergencies | Clinical judgment: Recognize Cues | Difficulty: hard_

**3.** A. Remain lying flat in bed for the period specified by facility protocol and increase oral fluid intake

Rationale: After a lumbar puncture, keeping the client flat/supine for the period specified by protocol (often several hours) and encouraging increased fluid intake helps replace cerebrospinal fluid volume and reduces the risk of a post-lumbar-puncture headache, which results from CSF leakage at the puncture site. Sitting upright (B) or ambulating immediately (D) increases pressure gradients that promote CSF leakage and headache, and restricting fluids (C) works against the goal of replacing lost CSF volume.

_Category: Reduction of Risk Potential — Potential for Complications of Diagnostic Tests/Treatments/Procedures | Clinical judgment: Take Action | Difficulty: easy_

**4.** D. Complete a focused suicide risk assessment, including current ideation, plan, and access to means → B. Conduct a thorough search of the client's belongings and person, removing sharps, cords, glass, medications, and other potentially dangerous items → C. Assign the client to a room close to the nursing station with the observation level (e.g., every-15-minute checks or continuous observation) indicated by the facility's protocol → A. Orient the client to the unit's rules, daily schedule, and expectations → E. Collaborate with the client to develop a written safety plan identifying triggers, coping strategies, and emergency contacts, such as the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline

Rationale: On admission of a client after a suicide attempt, the nurse should first complete a focused suicide risk assessment (ideation, plan, means) because this determines the level of precaution needed; next, physically remove dangerous items from the client's belongings and person to immediately reduce access to means; then assign a room and observation level based on the assessed risk; then orient the client to unit rules and the daily schedule once immediate safety needs are addressed; and finally, once the client is stabilized within the milieu, collaborate on a written safety plan with coping strategies and crisis resources, which is a more involved, forward-looking intervention best done after the acute admission tasks are complete. Performing the steps out of this order — for example, orienting the client to the unit before securing the environment and assessing risk — would leave the client in an unsafe environment longer than necessary.

_Category: Psychosocial Integrity — Therapeutic Environment | Clinical judgment: Take Action | Difficulty: hard_

**5.** B. Notify the provider immediately of findings consistent with rising intracranial pressure

Rationale: A widening pulse pressure (rising systolic with a stable or low diastolic), bradycardia, and irregular respirations constitute Cushing's triad, a late and ominous sign of rising intracranial pressure and impending brain herniation; the priority is immediate provider notification so emergency measures can be taken. Giving an antihypertensive would further compromise cerebral perfusion pressure. Coughing and straining raise intrathoracic and intracranial pressure and should be avoided. Lowering the head of the bed increases ICP rather than improving perfusion; the head should instead be kept elevated about 30 degrees.

_Category: Physiological Adaptation — Alterations in Body Systems | Clinical judgment: Analyze Cues | Difficulty: moderate_

**6.** B. The client is experiencing the perimenopausal transition, a normal midlife developmental change that commonly involves both physical symptoms and psychosocial reflection on roles and identity

Rationale: Irregular cycles, vasomotor symptoms such as hot flashes, and sleep disruption are classic physical features of the perimenopausal transition, and many women in midlife also reassess roles, identity, and relationships as children become more independent, a developmental transition sometimes referred to as the "empty nest" period. While thyroid dysfunction can cause overlapping symptoms and may be reasonable to rule out, stating the symptoms have "no relationship" to normal aging (A) is inaccurate given how classic this presentation is for perimenopause. The scenario does not describe the persistent low mood, anhedonia, or functional impairment required to diagnose a major depressive episode, so labeling this an emergency requiring hospitalization (C) is an overreaction. Perimenopause is a physiologic, hormonally driven process, not a condition caused solely by lifestyle choices (D).

_Category: Health Promotion and Maintenance — Developmental Stages and Transitions | Clinical judgment: Analyze Cues | Difficulty: hard_

**7.** B. Withhold the dose and notify the provider

Rationale: Nausea, anorexia, visual disturbances (yellow-green halos), and bradycardia (apical pulse <60 beats/min in an adult) are classic signs of digoxin toxicity. The nurse should withhold the dose and notify the provider so a digoxin level and potassium level can be drawn before any further dose is given. Administering the dose (A, D) could worsen life-threatening toxicity/dysrhythmias, and delaying notification to recheck later (C) wastes time when toxicity is already suspected.

_Category: Pharmacological and Parenteral Therapies — Adverse Effects/Contraindications/Side Effects/Interactions | Clinical judgment: Analyze Cues | Difficulty: moderate_

**8.** C. Treat the IV infiltration/hematoma per standard nursing care, since a DNR order applies only to cardiopulmonary resuscitation

Rationale: A do-not-resuscitate (DNR) order addresses only the withholding of cardiopulmonary resuscitation (chest compressions, defibrillation, intubation for cardiac or respiratory arrest); it does not limit routine nursing or medical care for unrelated, non-life-threatening problems. The nurse should treat the IV site complication using standard practice. Withholding all care (A), deferring to the ethics committee for a routine issue (B), or shifting the decision to family when the client is alert and has already expressed preferences via the directive (D) are all inappropriate.

_Category: Management of Care — Advance Directives | Clinical judgment: Take Action | Difficulty: moderate_

**9.** B. Stop the feeding, keep the head of the bed elevated (or position the client laterally if distress is present), assess respiratory status, and notify the provider

Rationale: Coughing, a sensation of tube displacement, and a large increase in residual volume are cues suggesting possible tube migration, delayed gastric emptying, and aspiration risk. The priority is to stop the feeding immediately, maintain a position that protects the airway (HOB elevated or side-lying if the client is in distress), assess respiratory status, and notify the provider before resuming. Continuing or increasing the rate (A, C) risks worsening aspiration, and blindly advancing the tube (D) without confirming placement is unsafe.

_Category: Basic Care and Comfort — Nutrition and Oral Hydration | Clinical judgment: Analyze Cues | Difficulty: hard_

**10.** C. Emergency treatment necessary to prevent harm may proceed under the emergency exception to informed consent while reasonable attempts continue to reach the parent/guardian

Rationale: The emergency exception to informed consent allows necessary emergent or urgent treatment of a minor to proceed without parental consent when a parent/guardian cannot be reached despite reasonable attempts, because delaying care could cause harm. Minors generally cannot independently consent to non-emergency treatment based on age alone (B), and requiring care to be delayed (A) or requiring security to physically produce a guardian before treating (D) could cause preventable harm and is not legally required under this exception.

_Category: Management of Care — Informed Consent | Clinical judgment: Take Action | Difficulty: moderate_

**11.** B. A client who requires routine oral medication administration and site care for a well-healing surgical incision

Rationale: Administering routine oral medications and performing wound/incision care are within the LPN/VN scope of practice in most jurisdictions but are outside the UAP scope, which is limited to basic hygiene, activities of daily living, ambulation, and collecting (not interpreting) data such as vital signs and intake/output. The bath, vital sign collection, and ambulation assistance are all appropriate UAP tasks because they are routine, non-invasive, and do not require nursing judgment.

_Category: Management of Care — Assignment/Delegation/Supervision | Clinical judgment: Prioritize Hypotheses | Difficulty: moderate_

**12.** A. Physical therapy for gait training and strength, B. Speech-language pathology for a swallow evaluation, C. Occupational therapy for activities of daily living retraining, D. Case management/social work for discharge planning and home care needs, F. Registered dietitian for a modified-consistency diet plan

Rationale: Physical therapy (A), occupational therapy (C), speech-language pathology for the dysphagia (B), case management/social work for discharge and home-care coordination (D), and dietary consultation for a swallow-safe diet (F) are all appropriate collaborative referrals for a stroke client with weakness and dysphagia. Routine respiratory therapy assessment (E) is not indicated because the scenario describes no respiratory impairment; adding an unnecessary consult does not reflect appropriate resource use.

_Category: Management of Care — Collaboration with Interdisciplinary Team | Clinical judgment: Generate Solutions | Difficulty: moderate_

**13.** A. "I will have blood drawn regularly to check my INR.", B. "I should keep my intake of leafy green vegetables consistent from week to week rather than avoiding them completely.", D. "I will use an electric razor instead of a straight razor when shaving.", E. "I should report bleeding gums, black stools, or unusual bruising to my provider."

Rationale: Warfarin requires routine INR monitoring (A) to keep the level in the therapeutic range. Vitamin K intake should be kept consistent, not eliminated, because sudden changes destabilize the INR (B). Bleeding precautions include using an electric razor (D) and reporting any signs of bleeding (E). NSAIDs such as ibuprofen (C) increase bleeding risk and should be avoided in favor of acetaminophen. Warfarin must never be stopped abruptly without provider guidance (F), as this can precipitate thromboembolic events.

_Category: Pharmacological and Parenteral Therapies — Adverse Effects/Contraindications/Side Effects/Interactions | Clinical judgment: Generate Solutions | Difficulty: moderate_

**14.** B. Secure the restraint straps using a quick-release knot that can be released rapidly in an emergency, C. Assess circulation, skin integrity, and neurovascular status at least every 2 hours, or more often per facility policy, D. Release one limb at a time on a rotating schedule to allow range of motion and repositioning, per facility policy, F. Document the client's behavior, the less restrictive measures attempted, and the client's response to the restraints

Rationale: Restraint straps must be secured to the stationary bed frame with a quick-release knot (B), never to a movable side rail (A), because raising or lowering the rail could tighten the strap and injure the client. The nurse must assess circulation, skin integrity, and neurovascular status at least every 2 hours or more frequently per facility policy (C), release each limb periodically to allow range of motion and reposition the client (D), and document the behavior, prior less restrictive interventions, and the client's response (F). Restraints must be reassessed regularly and discontinued as soon as the client no longer meets criteria, never continued indefinitely without reassessment (E) or used for staff convenience.

_Category: Safety and Infection Control — Use of Restraints/Safety Devices | Clinical judgment: Evaluate Outcomes | Difficulty: moderate_

**15.** B. Further assess the client for symptoms of postpartum depression and explore what support resources are realistically available to her

Rationale: Daily crying combined with a limited support system are risk factors for postpartum depression; the priority is to further assess the client's mood, sleep, and risk (including for self-harm or harm to the infant) and to explore what support resources — such as a postpartum support group, home-visiting services, or counseling — are actually available and acceptable to her. Reassuring the client that this is "normal" and will resolve on its own risks missing an evolving mood disorder that requires assessment and intervention. Suggesting the mother move in or that the husband change his work schedule proposes specific solutions before the nurse has even completed an assessment of the client's mental health status and without knowing whether these options are feasible for the family.

_Category: Psychosocial Integrity — Support Systems | Clinical judgment: Prioritize Hypotheses | Difficulty: moderate_

**16.** B. Wash the puncture site immediately with soap and water

Rationale: The immediate first action after a needlestick or sharps injury is to wash the exposed skin thoroughly with soap and water (or flush mucous membranes with water or saline) to reduce the pathogen load at the site. Reporting the exposure to employee/occupational health (A) is essential for post-exposure evaluation and prophylaxis but occurs after this initial first aid step. Applying a tourniquet (C) is not recommended and does not reduce infection risk. Aggressively squeezing the site (D) has no proven benefit and may cause additional tissue trauma.

_Category: Safety and Infection Control — Handling Hazardous/Infectious Materials | Clinical judgment: Recognize Cues | Difficulty: easy_

**17.** A. Assisting the client to ambulate in the hallway with a walker as tolerated, B. Measuring and recording intake and output, D. Reinforcing use of the continuous passive motion (CPM) machine as previously taught by the RN, E. Obtaining and recording routine vital signs, G. Assisting the client with hygiene and repositioning

Rationale: Ambulation assistance (A), intake and output measurement (B), reinforcement of a skill the RN already taught (D), routine vital sign collection (E), and hygiene/repositioning assistance (G) are all routine, non-judgment-based tasks within UAP scope for a stable client. Performing an initial or first-time assessment of a surgical dressing (C) requires nursing judgment and must be done by the RN, and teaching a client to use new equipment such as a PCA pump (F) is an independent nursing function that cannot be delegated.

_Category: Management of Care — Assignment/Delegation/Supervision | Clinical judgment: Generate Solutions | Difficulty: moderate_

**18.** C. Massage the fundus until it becomes firm

Rationale: A boggy, poorly contracted fundus signals uterine atony, the leading cause of early postpartum hemorrhage, so the nurse's immediate independent action is to massage the fundus until it firms up. Fundal displacement above the umbilicus and to one side classically indicates a full bladder pressing the uterus out of midline; if massage does not keep the fundus firm, the nurse should next have the client void or catheterize as needed, but reducing active hemorrhage risk with massage comes first. Administering oxytocin (B) may follow if massage alone is ineffective, and notifying the provider (A) or simply reassessing later (D) both delay an intervention the nurse can and should perform immediately.

_Category: Health Promotion and Maintenance — Ante/Intra/Postpartum and Newborn Care | Clinical judgment: Take Action | Difficulty: moderate_

**19.** A. Stop the infusion and notify the provider; anticipate restarting at a slower rate once symptoms resolve

Rationale: Flushing, pruritus, and hypotension shortly after starting a rapid vancomycin infusion are classic for 'red man syndrome,' a histamine-mediated infusion reaction rather than a true allergic reaction. The priority is to stop the infusion and notify the provider; symptoms typically resolve once the infusion is stopped, and subsequent doses are infused more slowly (over at least 60 minutes), sometimes with antihistamine pretreatment as ordered. Giving diphenhydramine while continuing the same rapid rate (B) does not remove the trigger and risks worsening hypotension. Increasing the rate (C) would worsen the reaction. This is not a benign, expected effect that can simply be monitored through (D) given the hemodynamic change.

_Category: Pharmacological and Parenteral Therapies — Adverse Effects/Contraindications/Side Effects/Interactions | Clinical judgment: Take Action | Difficulty: moderate_

**20.** A. A casualty breathing fewer than 30 breaths/min with a palpable radial pulse who is unresponsive to verbal commands and cannot follow simple instructions → C. A casualty with a deformed, swollen forearm who is alert, breathing normally, and can follow commands but is unable to walk → B. A casualty who can walk, is talking appropriately, and has only minor lacerations → D. A casualty who is not breathing even after the airway is repositioned and has no palpable pulse

Rationale: START triage prioritizes casualties by ability to walk, respiratory status, perfusion, and mental status. The casualty with abnormal respirations and an altered mental status who cannot follow commands, but still has a pulse, is classified Immediate/red and treated first (A). The casualty who cannot walk but is alert, breathing normally, and has a stable though painful injury is classified Delayed/yellow and treated second (C). The ambulatory casualty with only minor injuries is classified Minimal/green, the 'walking wounded,' and is treated after those with more serious injuries (B). The casualty with no respirations even after airway repositioning and no pulse is classified Expectant/black (deceased) in a mass casualty event, where resuscitation is not attempted so resources can be directed to salvageable casualties, placing this casualty last (D).

_Category: Safety and Infection Control — Emergency Response Plan | Clinical judgment: Prioritize Hypotheses | Difficulty: hard_

**21.** A. Cluster nursing care activities, such as vital signs, medication administration, and assessments, to minimize nighttime interruptions whenever possible

Rationale: Clustering care activities reduces the number of times the client is disturbed, preserving longer uninterrupted sleep periods and supporting normal sleep cycles. Bright overhead lighting (B) suppresses melatonin and disrupts circadian rhythm. Waking a client who is currently comfortable on a fixed schedule (C) causes unnecessary sleep fragmentation. Screen use close to bedtime (D) is stimulating and delays sleep onset rather than promoting it.

_Category: Basic Care and Comfort — Rest and Sleep | Clinical judgment: Generate Solutions | Difficulty: easy_

**22.** B. Naloxone

Rationale: Respiratory depression, pinpoint pupils, and decreased level of consciousness after opioid administration indicate opioid-induced respiratory depression, which is reversed with naloxone, an opioid antagonist. Flumazenil reverses benzodiazepines, protamine sulfate reverses heparin, and acetylcysteine treats acetaminophen overdose.

_Category: Pharmacological and Parenteral Therapies — Expected Actions/Outcomes | Clinical judgment: Take Action | Difficulty: easy_

**23.** B. Stop the antibiotic infusion and disconnect the IV tubing at the port closest to the client → A. Administer intramuscular epinephrine → C. Call for the rapid response/emergency team and prepare for possible advanced airway management → D. Give supplemental oxygen and place the client in a supine position with legs elevated as tolerated → E. Initiate a rapid infusion of IV isotonic fluids

Rationale: For suspected anaphylaxis, the first action is to stop the causative agent by discontinuing the infusion and disconnecting the tubing at the client so no further drug is delivered. Epinephrine IM is the priority life-saving medication and should be given immediately next, before other supportive measures. The nurse then calls for emergency assistance and prepares for a possible advanced airway, since laryngeal edema/stridor can progress to airway obstruction. Supplemental oxygen and positioning to support perfusion follow, and rapid IV fluid administration to treat hypotension/distributive shock comes after the airway and epinephrine priorities are addressed.

_Category: Physiological Adaptation — Medical Emergencies | Clinical judgment: Take Action | Difficulty: hard_

**24.** B. The findings are consistent with orthostatic hypotension; the nurse should implement fall precautions

Rationale: Orthostatic hypotension is defined as a drop in systolic blood pressure of 20 mmHg or more, or diastolic of 10 mmHg or more, within 3 minutes of standing, accompanied by symptoms such as dizziness; this client's systolic pressure dropped 24 mmHg with associated dizziness, meeting the criteria, and the nurse should implement fall precautions and notify the provider. The compensatory tachycardia does not make this a normal finding (A), the data do not reflect hydration status directly (C), and delaying intervention for 24 hours (D) places the client at immediate risk for a fall-related injury.

_Category: Reduction of Risk Potential — Vital Signs | Clinical judgment: Analyze Cues | Difficulty: moderate_

**25.** B. Tall, peaked T waves with widened QRS complex

Rationale: Missed dialysis leads to potassium retention and hyperkalemia. Classic ECG changes of hyperkalemia progress from tall, peaked T waves to a widened QRS complex and, if untreated, to a sine-wave pattern and cardiac arrest. Prolonged QT with flattened T waves and prominent U waves with ST depression are findings of hypokalemia, not hyperkalemia, and a shortened QT interval with tall P waves is not a recognized hyperkalemia pattern.

_Category: Physiological Adaptation — Fluid and Electrolyte Imbalances | Clinical judgment: Recognize Cues | Difficulty: moderate_
