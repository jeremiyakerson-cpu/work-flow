# Answer Key & Rationales

_NCLEX-RN Practice Test_

---

**1.** A. Obtain blood cultures from two different sites before starting antibiotics, B. Administer broad-spectrum IV antibiotics as soon as possible after cultures are drawn, C. Begin rapid IV isotonic fluid resuscitation, D. Measure serum lactate level

Rationale: Early recognition and management of sepsis focuses on obtaining blood cultures before starting antibiotics (so the causative organism can still be identified), giving broad-spectrum antibiotics as soon as possible afterward, beginning prompt IV fluid resuscitation to restore perfusion, and measuring serum lactate to assess tissue hypoperfusion and guide treatment. Antibiotics should never be withheld pending final culture results, as this delay increases mortality, and fluid resuscitation should begin promptly once hypoperfusion or hypotension is identified rather than waiting for further deterioration.

_Category: Physiological Adaptation — Illness Management | Clinical judgment: Prioritize Hypotheses | Difficulty: moderate_

**2.** 25 mL/hr

Rationale: Concentration = 20,000 units ÷ 500 mL = 40 units/mL. Rate = ordered dose ÷ concentration = 1,000 units/hr ÷ 40 units/mL = 25 mL/hr.

_Category: Pharmacological and Parenteral Therapies — Dosage Calculation | Clinical judgment: Foundational Knowledge | Difficulty: moderate_

**3.** B. Serum potassium 2.9 mEq/L

Rationale: Normal serum potassium is 3.5-5.0 mEq/L; a level of 2.9 mEq/L is significantly below normal and is a critical (panic) value, especially in a client on a potassium-wasting loop diuretic such as furosemide, because severe hypokalemia can precipitate life-threatening cardiac dysrhythmias and requires immediate provider notification. Sodium 138 mEq/L (normal 135-145), creatinine 1.0 mg/dL (normal 0.6-1.2), and glucose 98 mg/dL (normal fasting 70-100) are all within normal limits and do not require urgent notification.

_Category: Reduction of Risk Potential — Laboratory Values | Clinical judgment: Recognize Cues | Difficulty: easy_

**4.** B. Stop the antibiotic infusion and disconnect the IV tubing at the port closest to the client → A. Administer intramuscular epinephrine → C. Call for the rapid response/emergency team and prepare for possible advanced airway management → D. Give supplemental oxygen and place the client in a supine position with legs elevated as tolerated → E. Initiate a rapid infusion of IV isotonic fluids

Rationale: For suspected anaphylaxis, the first action is to stop the causative agent by discontinuing the infusion and disconnecting the tubing at the client so no further drug is delivered. Epinephrine IM is the priority life-saving medication and should be given immediately next, before other supportive measures. The nurse then calls for emergency assistance and prepares for a possible advanced airway, since laryngeal edema/stridor can progress to airway obstruction. Supplemental oxygen and positioning to support perfusion follow, and rapid IV fluid administration to treat hypotension/distributive shock comes after the airway and epinephrine priorities are addressed.

_Category: Physiological Adaptation — Medical Emergencies | Clinical judgment: Take Action | Difficulty: hard_

**5.** A. "I will have blood drawn regularly to check my INR.", B. "I should keep my intake of leafy green vegetables consistent from week to week rather than avoiding them completely.", D. "I will use an electric razor instead of a straight razor when shaving.", E. "I should report bleeding gums, black stools, or unusual bruising to my provider."

Rationale: Warfarin requires routine INR monitoring (A) to keep the level in the therapeutic range. Vitamin K intake should be kept consistent, not eliminated, because sudden changes destabilize the INR (B). Bleeding precautions include using an electric razor (D) and reporting any signs of bleeding (E). NSAIDs such as ibuprofen (C) increase bleeding risk and should be avoided in favor of acetaminophen. Warfarin must never be stopped abruptly without provider guidance (F), as this can precipitate thromboembolic events.

_Category: Pharmacological and Parenteral Therapies — Adverse Effects/Contraindications/Side Effects/Interactions | Clinical judgment: Generate Solutions | Difficulty: moderate_

**6.** B. A client who requires routine oral medication administration and site care for a well-healing surgical incision

Rationale: Administering routine oral medications and performing wound/incision care are within the LPN/VN scope of practice in most jurisdictions but are outside the UAP scope, which is limited to basic hygiene, activities of daily living, ambulation, and collecting (not interpreting) data such as vital signs and intake/output. The bath, vital sign collection, and ambulation assistance are all appropriate UAP tasks because they are routine, non-invasive, and do not require nursing judgment.

_Category: Management of Care — Assignment/Delegation/Supervision | Clinical judgment: Prioritize Hypotheses | Difficulty: moderate_

**7.** A. "Aim for at least 150 minutes per week of moderate-intensity aerobic activity, along with muscle-strengthening activity on 2 or more days a week."

Rationale: General evidence-based guidance for healthy adults recommends at least 150 minutes per week of moderate-intensity aerobic activity plus muscle-strengthening activity on 2 or more days per week, with benefits achievable through activities as simple as brisk walking. Requiring daily 5-mile runs (B) is an unrealistic and unnecessarily extreme threshold. Diet alone does not replace the cardiovascular, musculoskeletal, and mental health benefits of physical activity (C). Advising a client to avoid exercise until weight loss occurs (D) is inaccurate and counterproductive, since regular activity itself supports healthy weight management.

_Category: Health Promotion and Maintenance — Lifestyle Choices | Clinical judgment: Foundational Knowledge | Difficulty: easy_

**8.** B. Hypocalcemia

Rationale: A positive Chvostek's sign (facial muscle twitching when the facial nerve is tapped) along with perioral and fingertip tingling suggests hypocalcemia, a known risk after thyroidectomy if the parathyroid glands are inadvertently damaged or removed, since the parathyroid glands regulate calcium. Hypercalcemia produces the opposite picture (lethargy, weakness, decreased reflexes), and hypernatremia does not classically cause this sign. Hypomagnesemia can also cause a positive Chvostek's sign and is not ruled out by this finding, making that option incorrect as stated.

_Category: Physiological Adaptation — Fluid and Electrolyte Imbalances | Clinical judgment: Analyze Cues | Difficulty: moderate_

**9.** B. Instruct the client to go to the emergency department immediately

Rationale: Lip/tongue swelling and difficulty swallowing after starting an ACE inhibitor (such as lisinopril) are signs of angioedema, a rare but potentially life-threatening adverse effect that can progress to airway obstruction. The client needs emergency evaluation immediately; this is not a symptom to manage at home or dismiss as expected (a dry, nonproductive cough is the common/expected ACE inhibitor effect, not angioedema).

_Category: Pharmacological and Parenteral Therapies — Adverse Effects/Contraindications/Side Effects/Interactions | Clinical judgment: Analyze Cues | Difficulty: moderate_

**10.** B. Soap and water hand washing

Rationale: C. difficile produces spores that are resistant to alcohol-based hand hygiene products; only mechanical friction with soap and water reliably removes the spores from the hands. Alcohol-based gel or foam sanitizers (A, C), even antimicrobial ones, do not kill or adequately remove C. difficile spores. Gloves reduce contamination but do not eliminate the need for hand hygiene immediately after glove removal (D) — hands must always be washed.

_Category: Safety and Infection Control — Standard/Transmission-Based/Other Precautions | Clinical judgment: Analyze Cues | Difficulty: hard_

**11.** A. Coarse hand tremors, C. Diaphoresis and anxiety, D. Auditory or visual hallucinations, E. Heart rate 120/min with a blood pressure of 168/100 mm Hg, F. Generalized tonic-clonic seizure activity

Rationale: Alcohol withdrawal produces sympathetic (autonomic) hyperactivity and central nervous system excitability, presenting as coarse tremors, diaphoresis and anxiety, hallucinations (typically visual or tactile), tachycardia with hypertension, and, in more severe cases, generalized tonic-clonic seizures — seizures can occur roughly 12-48 hours after the last drink, and untreated withdrawal can progress to delirium tremens around 48-96 hours. Hypotension with bradycardia and somnolence with a decreased respiratory rate reflect central nervous system and cardiovascular depression, which is more consistent with intoxication or oversedation than withdrawal, so these two findings should not be attributed to withdrawal syndrome.

_Category: Psychosocial Integrity — Chemical/Other Dependency | Clinical judgment: Recognize Cues | Difficulty: hard_

**12.** B. A client with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease whose oxygen saturation dropped from 94% to 88% and is now audibly wheezing

Rationale: Using the airway-breathing-circulation framework, a client with a falling oxygen saturation and new audible wheezing has an acute, unstable respiratory change that could progress to respiratory failure and must be assessed first. The postoperative pain (A), teaching request (C), and discharge teaching (D) all involve stable clients whose needs, while important, are not immediately life-threatening.

_Category: Management of Care — Establishing Priorities | Clinical judgment: Prioritize Hypotheses | Difficulty: moderate_

**13.** B. Assess airway patency and the quality/rate of respirations → C. Assess vital signs, including heart rate, blood pressure, and oxygen saturation → D. Assess level of consciousness and orientation → E. Inspect the surgical dressing and any drains for bleeding or drainage → A. Assess pain level and administer analgesia as needed

Rationale: Using an airway-breathing-circulation-based priority framework, the nurse first assesses airway patency and respiratory status (B), since airway compromise is immediately life-threatening. Next, vital signs including circulation indicators such as heart rate, blood pressure, and oxygen saturation are assessed (C). Level of consciousness (D) is assessed next to evaluate neurologic recovery from anesthesia, followed by inspection of the surgical site and drains for bleeding (E). Pain assessment and management (A) is important but is addressed after the client's airway, circulation, neurologic status, and surgical site have been confirmed stable.

_Category: Reduction of Risk Potential — Potential for Complications from Surgical Procedures and Health Alterations | Clinical judgment: Prioritize Hypotheses | Difficulty: moderate_

**14.** B. "I should catheterize on a regular schedule, such as every 4 to 6 hours, and whenever I feel bladder fullness."

Rationale: Intermittent self-catheterization at home is typically performed using clean (not sterile) technique on a regular schedule, generally every 4-6 hours, and additionally whenever the client senses fullness, to keep bladder volumes low and reduce UTI and overdistension risk. Requiring full sterile technique at home (A) is unnecessary and impractical. Waiting up to 24 hours (C) or deliberately holding urine (D) allows bladder overdistension, which increases infection risk and can damage the detrusor muscle.

_Category: Basic Care and Comfort — Elimination | Clinical judgment: Foundational Knowledge | Difficulty: moderate_

**15.** B. "Discuss client information only in private areas, away from public hallways, elevators, and cafeterias.", C. "Log out of the electronic health record any time you step away from a workstation.", E. "Share client information with other staff only on a need-to-know basis related to that client's care.", F. "Shred or place in a designated bin any printed documents containing client identifiers before disposal."

Rationale: Protecting confidentiality requires discussing information only in private settings (B), logging out of unattended workstations (C), sharing information only on a need-to-know basis (E), and properly destroying printed materials with identifiers (F). Accessing a chart without a care-related need, even out of curiosity and even for a former client, is a HIPAA violation regardless of whether the information is shared further (A). Texting protected health information over an unsecured personal device is also a confidentiality/security violation, even if convenient (D).

_Category: Management of Care — Confidentiality/Information Security | Clinical judgment: Take Action | Difficulty: moderate_

**16.** C. Treat the IV infiltration/hematoma per standard nursing care, since a DNR order applies only to cardiopulmonary resuscitation

Rationale: A do-not-resuscitate (DNR) order addresses only the withholding of cardiopulmonary resuscitation (chest compressions, defibrillation, intubation for cardiac or respiratory arrest); it does not limit routine nursing or medical care for unrelated, non-life-threatening problems. The nurse should treat the IV site complication using standard practice. Withholding all care (A), deferring to the ethics committee for a routine issue (B), or shifting the decision to family when the client is alert and has already expressed preferences via the directive (D) are all inappropriate.

_Category: Management of Care — Advance Directives | Clinical judgment: Take Action | Difficulty: moderate_

**17.** B. Diabetic ketoacidosis

Rationale: Kussmaul respirations (deep, rapid breathing to blow off CO2), fruity/acetone breath odor, hyperglycemia, and a metabolic acidosis with low pH and low bicarbonate are hallmark findings of diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA), caused by absolute insulin deficiency and ketone body production, most common in type 1 diabetes. Hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state typically presents with much higher glucose levels, profound dehydration, and minimal to no ketoacidosis because some residual insulin is present. The Somogyi effect and dawn phenomenon describe patterns of morning hyperglycemia, not an acute acidotic crisis.

_Category: Physiological Adaptation — Pathophysiology | Clinical judgment: Analyze Cues | Difficulty: moderate_

**18.** C. Report the suspected abuse to adult protective services as required by law

Rationale: Nurses are mandated reporters; suspected abuse or neglect of a vulnerable adult (or child) must be reported to the appropriate protective services agency regardless of the client's wish for confidentiality, because the client's safety takes priority. Simply documenting without reporting (option 1) fails this legal and ethical duty. Confronting the caregiver directly (option 2) is not the nurse's role, could endanger the client further after the nurse leaves, and may compromise a subsequent investigation. Waiting to see if the pattern continues (option 4) delays a legally required report and leaves the client at continued risk of harm.

_Category: Psychosocial Integrity — Abuse/Neglect | Clinical judgment: Take Action | Difficulty: easy_

**19.** B. Stage 3 pressure injury

Rationale: A Stage 3 pressure injury involves full-thickness skin loss with visible adipose (fat) tissue; slough and/or eschar may be present but does not obscure the depth of tissue loss, and bone, tendon, and muscle are not exposed. Stage 2 involves only partial-thickness skin loss with exposed dermis (no fat visible). Stage 4 would show exposed bone, tendon, or muscle. Unstageable means slough or eschar obscures the wound bed so depth cannot be determined; here the depth is clearly visualized, so it is stageable as a Stage 3.

_Category: Basic Care and Comfort — Mobility/Immobility | Clinical judgment: Recognize Cues | Difficulty: easy_

**20.** B. Verify that the order specifies the type of restraint, the behavior necessitating it, and a time limit

Rationale: Restraints require a current, time-limited provider order (per facility policy, typically renewed at defined intervals, not to exceed 24 hours) that specifies the type of restraint and the specific behavior warranting its use; the nurse must confirm the order meets these criteria before applying the device. Less restrictive alternatives were already attempted, so restraints are appropriate here, but applying first and notifying later (A) reverses the correct sequence except in true self-limited emergencies where an order must still be obtained within a defined short window. Sedation alone (C) is a chemical restraint decision separate from the physical restraint order and is not a substitute for correct restraint procedure. Most facility policies do not require a separate signed family consent form before applying a clinically indicated restraint (D), though the family should be informed.

_Category: Safety and Infection Control — Use of Restraints/Safety Devices | Clinical judgment: Prioritize Hypotheses | Difficulty: hard_

**21.** C. Notify the charge nurse or supervisor of the practice limitation and request an appropriate reassignment or supervised orientation to the task

Rationale: A nurse is legally and ethically responsible for practicing only within their individual competency, even when floated to an unfamiliar unit. The appropriate action is to notify the supervisor of the specific skill or knowledge gap and request reassignment to tasks within the nurse's competency or supervised guidance for the unfamiliar task. Performing an unfamiliar task independently (A, D) risks client harm and exposes the nurse to liability, while refusing the entire assignment and leaving (B) is patient abandonment; the concern should be raised through proper channels instead.

_Category: Management of Care — Legal Rights and Responsibilities | Clinical judgment: Analyze Cues | Difficulty: moderate_

**22.** A. Assess the client's current knowledge, health literacy, and readiness to learn → C. Provide the information in short segments using clear, non-medical language and appropriate materials → B. Demonstrate the steps of blood glucose self-monitoring using the client's own equipment → D. Have the client perform a return demonstration using the teach-back method → E. Document the teaching provided and the client's demonstrated level of understanding

Rationale: Effective client teaching begins by assessing the client's baseline knowledge, health literacy, and readiness to learn, since this determines how the content should be paced and presented. Next, the nurse delivers the information in manageable segments using plain language appropriate to the client's literacy level, followed by a hands-on demonstration of the actual skill using the client's own supplies. The client then performs a return demonstration/teach-back so the nurse can verify understanding, and finally the nurse documents the teaching provided and the client's demonstrated competence. Performing the steps out of this order — for example, demonstrating or testing understanding before assessing readiness and providing information — undermines learning and produces an inaccurate evaluation.

_Category: Health Promotion and Maintenance — Self-Care | Clinical judgment: Take Action | Difficulty: moderate_

**23.** A. Use the incentive spirometer approximately 10 times every hour while awake, B. Ambulate as soon as the provider allows, starting the day of or day after surgery, C. Wear sequential compression devices whenever not ambulating, E. Cough and deep breathe every 1-2 hours while awake, G. Perform ankle pumps and calf-tightening exercises while in bed

Rationale: Incentive spirometry (A) and coughing/deep breathing (E) expand the lungs and mobilize secretions to prevent atelectasis. Early ambulation (B), sequential compression devices when not ambulating (C), and ankle pumps/calf exercises (G) promote venous return and reduce venous stasis, lowering DVT risk. Prolonged bed rest (D) increases, rather than decreases, the risk of both DVT and atelectasis, and crossing the legs (F) compresses the popliteal vessels and impairs venous return, increasing DVT risk.

_Category: Reduction of Risk Potential — Potential for Alterations in Body Systems | Clinical judgment: Generate Solutions | Difficulty: moderate_

**24.** B. Withhold the dose and notify the provider

Rationale: Nausea, anorexia, visual disturbances (yellow-green halos), and bradycardia (apical pulse <60 beats/min in an adult) are classic signs of digoxin toxicity. The nurse should withhold the dose and notify the provider so a digoxin level and potassium level can be drawn before any further dose is given. Administering the dose (A, D) could worsen life-threatening toxicity/dysrhythmias, and delaying notification to recheck later (C) wastes time when toxicity is already suspected.

_Category: Pharmacological and Parenteral Therapies — Adverse Effects/Contraindications/Side Effects/Interactions | Clinical judgment: Analyze Cues | Difficulty: moderate_

**25.** A. Wear a surgical mask when within 3 to 6 feet of the client, B. Place the client in a private room, or cohort with another client with the same confirmed infection, per facility policy, D. Perform hand hygiene before and after client contact, E. Instruct visitors to wear a surgical mask when in the client's room

Rationale: Droplet precautions are used for organisms spread by large respiratory droplets (e.g., influenza, pertussis, mumps, meningococcus) that travel only short distances. A surgical mask worn within about 3-6 feet of the client (A) is sufficient — an N95 respirator (C) is reserved for airborne precautions and is not required here. A private room or cohorting with a client who has the same confirmed infection (B), hand hygiene before and after contact (D), and having visitors mask in the room (E) are all appropriate. If the client must leave the room, the client should wear a surgical mask (not go unmasked, F) to protect others in transit.

_Category: Safety and Infection Control — Standard/Transmission-Based/Other Precautions | Clinical judgment: Take Action | Difficulty: moderate_
