# Domain 3 — Trauma & Multisystem Shock (topics 0-2)

@ 3.0
? A motorcyclist arrives with bright red blood spurting from a mangled lower leg. He is moaning and breathing spontaneously. What is the first action in the primary survey?
+ Control the bleeding with direct pressure or a tourniquet
- Apply a cervical collar and secure him to a long backboard | Spinal precautions matter, but exsanguination kills first.
- Insert two large-bore IV lines | Access cannot replace blood that is still being lost.
- Obtain a full set of vital signs | Measurement does not treat an immediately lethal problem.
= Obvious life-threatening external hemorrhage is controlled first, before the airway is addressed.

? An unresponsive driver from a rollover crash has gurgling respirations. How should the nurse open the airway?
+ Jaw thrust with manual stabilization of the cervical spine
- Head tilt with chin lift | Extending the neck may worsen an unstable cervical injury.
- Turning the head to one side | Rotation moves a possibly injured spine.
- Flexing the neck forward on a pillow to bring the chin down | Flexion narrows the airway and endangers the cord.
= In trauma the airway is opened with a jaw thrust while the head is held in neutral alignment, then suctioned.

? A trauma patient opens his eyes only to pain, makes incomprehensible sounds and withdraws from pain. What is his Glasgow Coma Scale score, and what does it mean for the airway?
+ 8; a definitive airway is needed
- 11; observation is adequate | The score is overcounted.
- 5; care should be withdrawn | The score is undercounted and does not decide prognosis.
- 13; a nasal cannula is adequate | The score is overcounted and the airway is at risk.
= Eyes 2, verbal 2 and motor 4 total 8; a score of 8 or less usually means the airway cannot be protected.

? During the exposure step of the primary survey, the patient's clothing has been cut away and injuries inspected. What must the nurse do next?
+ Cover the patient with warm blankets and warm the room
- Leave the patient uncovered for repeat inspection | Prolonged exposure causes hypothermia.
- Apply ice packs to all contusions | Cooling adds to heat loss in a trauma patient.
- Give room-temperature fluids rapidly | Unwarmed fluids lower the core temperature.
= Trauma patients lose heat quickly, and hypothermia worsens coagulopathy and acidosis, so warming follows exposure at once.

? While performing the secondary survey the nurse finds that the patient has become newly confused with a falling blood pressure. What is the correct response?
+ Stop and return to the primary survey
- Finish the head-to-toe assessment first | A change in condition takes priority over completing the survey.
- Record the change and reassess in 15 minutes | Deterioration needs immediate re-evaluation.
- Move directly to the CT scanner | An unstable patient should be reassessed and stabilized first.
= Any deterioration prompts immediate reassessment of airway, breathing, circulation and disability.

? Which combination is known as the lethal triad of trauma?
+ Hypothermia, acidosis and coagulopathy
- Hypertension, bradycardia and irregular breathing | That is Cushing's triad of raised intracranial pressure.
- Hypotension, muffled heart sounds and distended neck veins | That is Beck's triad of tamponade.
- Fever, jaundice and right upper quadrant pain | That is Charcot's triad of cholangitis.
= Hypothermia, acidosis and coagulopathy reinforce one another and drive mortality in the bleeding patient.

? A patient with facial fractures and clear fluid draining from the nose needs gastric decompression. Which route should the nurse use?
+ Orogastric
- Nasogastric | A nasal tube can pass through a fractured cribriform plate into the cranium.
- Nasoduodenal | Any nasal route carries the same risk.
- No decompression is ever allowed | Decompression is permitted by the oral route.
= Midface or suspected basilar skull fractures contraindicate nasal tubes; the oral route is used.

? A man who fell astride a beam has blood at the urethral meatus and a scrotal hematoma. A urinary catheter is ordered. What should the nurse do?
+ Withhold insertion and anticipate a retrograde urethrogram
- Insert a smaller catheter gently | Any blind catheterization can complete a partial urethral tear.
- Insert the catheter and report bleeding afterwards | The damage would already be done.
- Ask the patient to bear down during insertion | Straining does not make insertion safe.
= Blood at the meatus, perineal or scrotal hematoma or a high-riding prostate suggest urethral injury, which is imaged before catheterization.

? What is the main purpose of log-rolling a trauma patient during the secondary survey?
+ To inspect and palpate the back while keeping the spine aligned
- To check the patient's ability to cooperate and follow instructions | Cooperation is not the purpose.
- To drain blood from the chest | Positioning is not used to drain a hemothorax.
- To relieve pressure on the abdomen | That is not the reason for the maneuver.
= The posterior surfaces hide wounds, deformity and spinal tenderness; the roll is performed with the spine held in line.

? An adult struck by a car is alert with stable vital signs. Which history element is most useful for predicting hidden injuries?
+ Mechanism of injury, such as vehicle speed and point of impact
- The patient's occupation | Occupation does not predict injury pattern.
- The date of the last tetanus booster and any reactions to vaccines | Tetanus status matters later for wounds.
- The patient's insurance carrier | It has no clinical relevance.
= Mechanism and energy transfer guide the search for occult injuries when the initial examination is reassuring.

@ 3.1
? A chainsaw injury to the thigh continues to bleed briskly through a pressure dressing held firmly for several minutes. What is the next step?
+ Apply a tourniquet above the wound
- Remove the dressing to inspect the wound again | Lifting the dressing disrupts any clot that has formed.
- Elevate the leg and continue as before | Elevation alone will not stop arterial bleeding.
- Apply a cold pack over the dressing | Cold does not control arterial hemorrhage.
= When direct pressure fails to control life-threatening extremity bleeding, a tourniquet is applied.

? Where and how should a limb tourniquet be applied?
+ Two to three inches above the wound, tightened until bleeding stops
- Directly over the wound, tightened until the patient finds it uncomfortable | It must be proximal and tight enough to occlude arterial flow.
- Over the nearest joint for better grip | Joints prevent effective compression of the artery.
- Loosely, so venous return is preserved | A loose tourniquet blocks veins only and increases bleeding.
= A tourniquet goes proximal to the wound, not over a joint, and is tightened until bleeding stops; the time is recorded.

? Twenty minutes after a tourniquet controlled a brachial artery bleed, the patient begs to have it loosened because of pain. What should the nurse do?
+ Leave it in place and treat the pain
- Loosen it briefly every ten minutes | Intermittent release causes rebleeding and clot loss.
- Remove it and reapply pressure dressings | Removal belongs to the surgical team once control is possible.
- Move it further down the arm | Repositioning distal to the wound would not control bleeding.
= Tourniquet pain is expected; the device stays on until definitive hemorrhage control is available.

? A stab wound high in the groin is bleeding heavily and is too proximal for a tourniquet. Which technique should be used?
+ Pack the wound tightly with hemostatic gauze and hold firm pressure
- Cover the wound with a loose sterile dressing and elevate the pelvis | A loose dressing exerts no pressure on the vessel.
- Apply a tourniquet around the waist | A tourniquet cannot compress junctional vessels.
- Clamp blindly into the wound | Blind clamping injures nerves and vessels.
= Junctional bleeding in the groin, axilla or neck is controlled by deep wound packing and sustained direct pressure.

? A massive transfusion protocol is activated for a patient with a shattered pelvis. In what ratio should blood components be given?
+ About 1 red cell unit to 1 plasma to 1 platelet
- 6 red cell units before any plasma | Delaying plasma and platelets worsens dilutional coagulopathy.
- Plasma only until the laboratory results return | Red cells are needed for oxygen delivery.
- 3 L of crystalloid for every red cell unit | Large crystalloid volumes dilute clotting factors.
= Balanced resuscitation approximating whole blood (1:1:1) reduces death from hemorrhage.

? A patient with hemorrhagic shock arrives 90 minutes after injury. Which medication should the nurse anticipate to reduce bleeding?
+ Tranexamic acid
- Vitamin K | Vitamin K acts slowly and treats warfarin effect.
- Protamine sulfate | Protamine reverses heparin.
- Desmopressin as the first-line agent | It is not the standard antifibrinolytic in trauma.
= Tranexamic acid inhibits clot breakdown and lowers mortality when given within three hours of injury.

? During a massive transfusion the patient develops perioral tingling, hypotension and a prolonged QT interval. What is the likely cause?
+ Hypocalcemia from the citrate in stored blood
- Hyperkalemia from red cell breakdown | Hyperkalemia peaks T waves rather than lengthening the QT.
- Hypermagnesemia | Magnesium excess is not a transfusion effect.
- Fluid overload | Overload causes crackles and hypertension, not these findings.
= Citrate binds ionized calcium; rapid transfusion requires calcium monitoring and replacement.

? A patient with a penetrating abdominal wound is alert with BP 86/54 while the operating room is prepared. What is the appropriate fluid strategy?
+ Limit fluids and accept a low-normal pressure until bleeding is controlled
- Give warmed crystalloid rapidly until the systolic pressure is above 120 mmHg | Raising pressure dislodges clots and dilutes clotting factors.
- Withhold all blood products until surgery | Blood is the appropriate resuscitation fluid.
- Start a vasopressor to normalize pressure | Pressors do not replace volume and worsen tissue perfusion.
= Permissive hypotension in penetrating torso trauma limits bleeding until surgical control, provided there is no head injury.

? A 24-year-old woman in hemorrhagic shock needs blood before her type is known. Which red cells should be issued?
+ O negative
- O positive | Rh-positive blood risks sensitizing a woman of childbearing age.
- AB positive | AB red cells are compatible only with AB recipients.
- A negative | Type A cells are unsafe for an unknown recipient.
= O-negative red cells are given to females of childbearing potential to avoid Rh sensitization.

? Which finding is the earliest sign of hemorrhagic shock in a previously healthy adult?
+ Tachycardia with a narrowed pulse pressure
- A systolic pressure below 90 mmHg | Hypotension appears only after about 30% of blood volume is lost.
- Absent urine output | Anuria is a late sign.
- Unresponsiveness | Coma indicates profound, late shock.
= Compensation maintains systolic pressure early; rising heart rate and narrowing pulse pressure appear first.

@ 3.2
? A patient stabbed in the right chest has severe dyspnea, BP 76/40, distended neck veins and absent breath sounds on the right. What should the nurse prepare for immediately?
+ Needle decompression of the right chest
- A portable chest radiograph to confirm | The diagnosis is clinical and waiting for imaging can be fatal.
- Pericardiocentesis | Unilateral absent breath sounds point to the pleura, not the pericardium.
- Rapid sequence intubation first | Positive pressure will worsen an undrained tension pneumothorax.
= Tension pneumothorax is treated on clinical findings with immediate decompression, followed by a chest tube.
! Preferred adult decompression site (4th-5th intercostal space anterior to the mid-axillary line vs 2nd intercostal space mid-clavicular) varies by course edition.

? A patient has a gunshot wound to the chest that makes a sucking sound with each breath. Which dressing is appropriate?
+ A vented chest seal or an occlusive dressing taped on three sides
- A dry gauze pad taped loosely | Gauze lets air continue to enter the pleural space.
- A fully sealed airtight dressing taped down securely on all four sides | A complete seal can convert the wound to a tension pneumothorax.
- A wet-to-dry dressing | It does not prevent air entry.
= A one-way seal stops air entering during inspiration while letting it escape during expiration.

? After an occlusive dressing is applied to an open chest wound, the patient becomes more dyspneic and hypotensive with tracheal deviation. What should the nurse do first?
+ Lift one edge of the dressing to release trapped air
- Tape the dressing down more firmly | A tighter seal traps more air.
- Place the patient flat with the legs raised on pillows | Positioning does not relieve intrapleural pressure.
- Give a sedative for anxiety | The distress has a mechanical cause.
= Trapped air under a sealed dressing causes tension physiology; briefly opening the seal lets it escape.

? A chest tube placed for a hemothorax drains 1,600 mL of blood immediately. What does the nurse anticipate?
+ Preparation for the operating room
- Clamping the tube to slow the loss | Clamping hides ongoing bleeding and compresses the lung.
- Removing the chest tube | The tube is needed to monitor output.
- Routine observation on a general unit | This volume signals a surgical bleed.
= Initial output over 1,500 mL, or continuing loss above 200 mL per hour, indicates massive hemothorax needing thoracotomy.

? A segment of the left chest wall moves inward on inspiration and outward on expiration after a crash. What is the priority for this patient?
+ Oxygen, effective analgesia and monitoring for respiratory failure
- Sandbags placed on the loose segment | External splinting restricts ventilation.
- Tight circumferential chest strapping to hold the loose segment still | Binding promotes atelectasis and pneumonia.
- Fluid boluses to expand the lungs | Excess fluid worsens the underlying contusion.
= Flail chest impairs ventilation mainly through pain and the pulmonary contusion beneath it.

? A patient with a pulmonary contusion had SpO2 97% on arrival. Eight hours later it is 89% with increasing work of breathing. How should the nurse interpret this?
+ Expected progression of the contusion that needs escalation of respiratory support
- A sign that the pulse oximeter is faulty | The change fits the natural course of contusion.
- Anxiety that needs reassurance only | Falling saturation has a physical cause.
- Evidence that too little IV fluid was given during the first hours of resuscitation | Extra fluid aggravates contused lung.
= Pulmonary contusion worsens over 24-48 hours as edema and hemorrhage fill the alveoli.

? The nurse notes continuous bubbling in the water-seal chamber of a chest drainage system. What does this indicate?
+ An air leak in the patient or the system
- Normal function of the suction control | Suction bubbling occurs in the suction chamber, not the water seal.
- A blocked chest tube | Obstruction stops fluctuation rather than causing bubbling.
- Full re-expansion of the lung | Re-expansion ends bubbling and tidaling.
= Continuous water-seal bubbling means air is entering the system; connections and the insertion site are checked.

? A driver whose chest struck the steering wheel has a sternal fracture and new frequent premature ventricular contractions. What should the nurse suspect?
+ Blunt cardiac injury
- Costochondritis | Inflammation of cartilage does not cause dysrhythmias.
- Esophageal rupture | Rupture causes mediastinal air and fever, not ectopy.
- Gastroesophageal reflux | Reflux does not follow blunt trauma in this way.
= Anterior chest impact can bruise the myocardium; a 12-lead ECG and cardiac monitoring are indicated.

? A patient who fell 30 feet has chest pain, a widened mediastinum on chest radiograph and unequal arm pressures. Which injury is most likely?
+ Traumatic aortic injury
- Simple rib fracture | Rib fractures do not widen the mediastinum.
- Tracheobronchial tear | That causes air leak and subcutaneous emphysema.
- Diaphragmatic rupture | That shows bowel in the chest.
= Rapid deceleration tears the aorta near the ligamentum arteriosum, widening the mediastinum.

? An 81-year-old has four right-sided rib fractures after a fall. Which intervention best prevents complications?
+ Adequate analgesia with deep breathing and incentive spirometry
- A rib belt to limit chest movement | Binders reduce ventilation and promote pneumonia.
- Strict bed rest for a week | Immobility increases pulmonary complications.
- Withholding all opioids to avoid any risk of sedation or confusion | Uncontrolled pain causes splinting and atelectasis.
= Older adults with multiple rib fractures are at high risk of pneumonia; pain control allows deep breathing and coughing.
