# Domain 6 — Neurological Emergencies (topics 0-2)

@ 6.0
? A 68-year-old arrives with right-sided weakness and slurred speech. Which piece of history is most important for treatment decisions?
+ The time he was last known to be well
- The time his wife first noticed the symptoms | Discovery time may be hours after onset.
- His usual blood pressure medication | Useful, but it does not set treatment eligibility.
- Whether a parent had a stroke | Family history does not guide acute treatment.
= Eligibility for thrombolysis and thrombectomy is timed from when the patient was last known to be normal.

? A patient is brought in as a possible stroke with facial droop and confusion. Which test should be done first at the bedside?
+ A capillary blood glucose
- A fasting lipid panel sent urgently | Lipids do not affect acute decisions.
- A urinalysis | It does not identify a stroke mimic quickly.
- A troponin | It can follow but is not the first priority.
= Hypoglycemia mimics stroke and is corrected in minutes; glucose is the only laboratory value required before thrombolysis in most patients.

? What is the main purpose of the emergent non-contrast head CT in a suspected stroke?
+ To exclude intracranial hemorrhage
- To confirm an ischemic infarct | Early ischemia is often not visible on CT.
- To measure intracranial pressure | CT does not measure pressure.
- To identify the embolic source | CT of the head does not show the source.
= Hemorrhage must be excluded before thrombolysis; a normal early CT is consistent with ischemic stroke.

? Within what time from last known well is IV thrombolysis generally given for acute ischemic stroke?
+ 4.5 hours
- 1 hour | The window is considerably longer than one hour.
- 12 hours | That is beyond the standard thrombolysis window.
- 24 hours | That figure applies to selected thrombectomy candidates.
= IV thrombolysis is given within 4.5 hours of last known well in eligible patients; earlier treatment gives better outcomes.

? A woman went to bed normal at 22:00 and woke at 06:00 with left arm weakness. What is her last-known-well time?
+ 22:00
- 06:00 | Waking time is when symptoms were discovered, not when they began.
- 02:00, the midpoint | The midpoint is not used.
- It cannot be determined, so no workup is needed | The workup proceeds, and advanced imaging may identify candidates.
= For wake-up strokes the last-known-well time is when the patient was last seen normal; imaging can identify some who still benefit from treatment.

? An 80 kg patient is to receive alteplase 0.9 mg/kg for ischemic stroke. What are the total dose and the bolus?
+ 72 mg total, with 7.2 mg as a bolus over one minute
- 90 mg in total, with 9 mg given as a bolus over one minute | 90 mg is the maximum, reached only at 100 kg or more.
- 72 mg total, all given as a bolus | Only 10% is given as a bolus.
- 7.2 mg total as an infusion | That is only the bolus portion.
= Alteplase for stroke is 0.9 mg/kg to a maximum of 90 mg: 10% as a bolus and the remainder over 60 minutes.

? A patient has just finished an alteplase infusion for ischemic stroke. Which order should the nurse question?
+ Aspirin 325 mg now
- Neurologic checks every 15 minutes | Frequent checks are required.
- Blood pressure checks every 15 minutes | Frequent checks are required.
- Bed rest with the patient kept NPO until a swallow screen | That is appropriate.
= Antiplatelets and anticoagulants are withheld for 24 hours after thrombolysis, until follow-up imaging excludes hemorrhage.

? Forty minutes into an alteplase infusion a patient develops a sudden severe headache, vomits and becomes drowsy. What should the nurse do first?
+ Stop the infusion
- Give an antiemetic and continue | These signs suggest intracranial bleeding.
- Slow the infusion by half | Any further drug can worsen bleeding.
- Wait for the infusion to finish and then call | Delay allows the hemorrhage to expand.
= New headache, vomiting or neurologic decline during thrombolysis suggests intracranial hemorrhage; the infusion is stopped and an emergent CT obtained.

? During alteplase a patient who takes lisinopril develops swelling of the tongue and one side of the lips. What is the priority?
+ Stop the infusion and prepare to manage the airway
- Continue, since swelling is expected | Orolingual angioedema can progress to obstruction.
- Apply a cold compress and recheck in an hour | Local measures do not protect the airway.
- Give oral diphenhydramine and continue | Oral medication is inadequate and the drug should stop.
= Orolingual angioedema is a recognized reaction to alteplase, more common in patients on ACE inhibitors.

? A patient arrives 9 hours after onset with dense left hemiplegia, right gaze deviation and neglect. CT angiography shows a middle cerebral artery occlusion. What should the nurse anticipate?
+ Evaluation for mechanical thrombectomy
- Comfort care, since the thrombolysis window has passed | Thrombectomy has a longer window in selected patients.
- IV alteplase now | The standard thrombolysis window has passed.
- Discharge with antiplatelet therapy | A large vessel occlusion needs emergent intervention.
= Selected patients with large vessel occlusion benefit from thrombectomy up to 24 hours from last known well.
! Late-window thrombectomy selection criteria were not re-checked against the 2026 AHA/ASA guideline text.

? A man had 15 minutes of right arm weakness and trouble speaking that has completely resolved. He wants to go home. What should the nurse explain?
+ The risk of a stroke is highest in the next few days, so urgent evaluation is needed
- He is fine because the symptoms resolved | A transient ischemic attack is a warning of impending stroke.
- He needs a routine neurology clinic appointment in about three months if it happens again | Evaluation should occur within hours to days.
- The episode was most likely anxiety | Focal deficits are not explained by anxiety.
= After a TIA the risk of stroke is greatest in the first 48 hours; rapid workup and antiplatelet therapy reduce it.

? A patient with ischemic stroke is otherwise eligible for alteplase. Which laboratory result excludes treatment?
+ An INR of 2.4 in a patient taking warfarin
- A glucose of 148 mg/dL | That value does not exclude treatment.
- A platelet count of 210,000/mm3 | That count is normal.
- A creatinine of 1.3 mg/dL | Mild renal impairment does not exclude treatment.
= An INR above 1.7 is a contraindication to IV thrombolysis because of the risk of intracranial hemorrhage.
! INR above 1.7 is the long-standing exclusion; not confirmed against the 2026 AHA/ASA guideline text.

@ 6.1
? A 44-year-old describes the sudden onset of "the worst headache of my life" while lifting weights, followed by vomiting and neck stiffness. What should the nurse suspect?
+ Subarachnoid hemorrhage
- Tension headache | Tension headaches are gradual and band-like.
- Migraine without aura | Migraine builds over minutes to hours and is usually recurrent.
- Sinusitis | Sinus pain is facial and positional.
= A thunderclap headache that peaks within seconds to a minute, with meningism, is subarachnoid hemorrhage until proven otherwise.

? A patient with a thunderclap headache 12 hours ago has a normal non-contrast head CT. Suspicion remains high. Which test should the nurse anticipate?
+ Lumbar puncture
- An electroencephalogram | EEG evaluates seizures.
- Skull radiographs | They do not detect hemorrhage.
- A repeat CT in one week | Delay risks a fatal rebleed.
= CT sensitivity falls with time; cerebrospinal fluid showing xanthochromia or persistent red cells identifies a missed hemorrhage.

? Why is nimodipine given to patients with aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage?
+ To reduce delayed cerebral ischemia from vasospasm
- To dissolve the clot around the aneurysm | It has no thrombolytic action.
- To lower intracranial pressure directly | It does not reduce intracranial pressure.
- To prevent seizures | It is not an anticonvulsant.
= Nimodipine improves outcome by reducing delayed ischemia, which peaks about 4 to 14 days after the bleed.

? A patient with an unsecured ruptured aneurysm is waiting for neurosurgery. Which nursing measure helps prevent rebleeding?
+ A quiet, dimly lit room with stool softeners and pain control
- Encouraging vigorous coughing | Coughing raises intracranial pressure.
- Frequent ambulation in the corridor | Exertion raises blood pressure.
- Keeping the patient flat in bed with the neck flexed on pillows | Neck flexion impairs venous drainage.
= Reducing stimulation and straining limits surges in blood pressure that can rupture the aneurysm again.

? A patient taking warfarin has an acute intracerebral hemorrhage and an INR of 3.6. Which treatment should the nurse prepare?
+ Four-factor prothrombin complex concentrate with IV vitamin K
- Protamine sulfate | Protamine reverses heparin.
- A platelet transfusion alone, repeated until the bleeding stops | Platelets do not correct warfarin effect.
- Oral vitamin K alone | Oral vitamin K takes many hours.
= Prothrombin complex concentrate reverses warfarin within minutes; vitamin K sustains the reversal.

? A patient taking dabigatran has a subdural hematoma. Which reversal agent is specific for this drug?
+ Idarucizumab
- Vitamin K | Vitamin K does not reverse direct thrombin inhibitors.
- Protamine sulfate | Protamine reverses heparin.
- Tranexamic acid alone | It is not a specific reversal agent.
= Idarucizumab is a monoclonal antibody fragment that binds dabigatran and reverses its effect immediately.

? A hypertensive patient has sudden headache, vomiting, right hemiplegia and a falling level of consciousness. A colleague says it is "obviously a bleed" and wants to lower the pressure aggressively before imaging. What is the best response?
+ Ischemic and hemorrhagic stroke cannot be reliably told apart without imaging
- Agree, because vomiting proves hemorrhage | Vomiting occurs in both types.
- Agree, because a blood pressure this high proves that the stroke is a hemorrhage | Blood pressure is elevated in both types.
- Suggest thrombolysis instead | Thrombolysis is never given before imaging.
= Clinical features overlap; a non-contrast CT distinguishes the two before blood pressure targets and treatment are chosen.

? A patient with a cerebellar hemorrhage has vertigo, vomiting and ataxia and is becoming drowsy. Why is urgent neurosurgical consultation needed?
+ Swelling in the posterior fossa can compress the brainstem quickly
- Cerebellar bleeds always stop on their own | Deterioration can be rapid and fatal.
- Surgery is the only way to control the vomiting caused by the vertigo | Vomiting is a symptom, not the indication.
- The cerebellum controls blood pressure | That is not its role.
= The posterior fossa has little room; cerebellar hematomas cause brainstem compression and hydrocephalus and often need surgical evacuation.

? A patient with subarachnoid hemorrhage has an external ventricular drain. At what anatomic level should the transducer be zeroed?
+ The tragus of the ear
- The sternal angle | That is not the reference for the ventricles.
- The mid-axillary line at the fourth intercostal space | That is the reference for cardiac pressures.
- The bridge of the nose | That is not the standard reference.
= The tragus approximates the foramen of Monro; leveling elsewhere causes over- or under-drainage.

? Which finding in a patient with an intracerebral hemorrhage indicates deterioration that needs immediate reporting?
+ A fall in Glasgow Coma Scale score from 14 to 11
- A stable headache rated 4/10 | A stable mild headache is expected.
- A request for the room lights to be dimmed | Photophobia is common and not a sign of decline.
- A blood pressure within the ordered range | That is the goal.
= A drop of two or more points in the Glasgow Coma Scale suggests hematoma expansion or rising intracranial pressure.

? A 74-year-old has a new temporal headache, scalp tenderness when combing her hair, jaw pain when chewing and blurred vision in one eye. What should the nurse anticipate?
+ High-dose corticosteroids started without delay
- A triptan for migraine | Triptans do not treat arteritis and risk vascular events.
- Antibiotics for sinusitis | The features are not infectious.
- Discharge with an analgesic and a clinic visit next month | Delay risks permanent blindness.
= Giant cell arteritis threatens vision; steroids are started on suspicion, before biopsy confirmation.

? A 35-year-old man has excruciating stabbing pain behind one eye with tearing and nasal congestion on the same side, occurring nightly for a week. Which treatment gives the fastest relief?
+ High-flow oxygen by non-rebreather mask
- Oral acetaminophen | It acts too slowly and is ineffective for this pain.
- A dark, quiet room and a period of sleep | Patients are restless and rest does not abort the attack.
- An oral opioid | Opioids are not effective for this headache.
= Cluster headache responds to 100% oxygen at high flow or a parenteral triptan.

@ 6.2
? A patient in the waiting room has a generalized tonic-clonic seizure. What should the nurse do first?
+ Protect the head and turn the patient onto the side
- Place a bite block between the teeth to protect the tongue | Objects in the mouth cause injury and obstruction.
- Restrain the arms and legs | Restraint causes fractures and dislocations.
- Give oral glucose gel | Nothing is given by mouth during a seizure.
= The priority is protection from injury and airway positioning; the seizure is timed and nothing is placed in the mouth.

? Which situation meets the definition of status epilepticus?
+ A convulsion lasting more than five minutes
- A 90-second seizure followed by drowsiness | That is a typical self-limited seizure.
- Two seizures a week apart | Separate events with recovery are not status.
- A brief staring spell | A single absence episode is not status.
= Status epilepticus is continuous seizure activity for five minutes or more, or repeated seizures without recovery between them.

? A patient has been convulsing for six minutes and has no IV access. Which medication should the nurse anticipate?
+ Intramuscular midazolam
- Oral levetiracetam | Oral drugs cannot be given during a seizure.
- Intramuscular phenytoin | Phenytoin is erratically absorbed and damages muscle.
- Rectal acetaminophen | It has no anticonvulsant effect.
= Benzodiazepines are first-line; when IV access is not available, IM midazolam is as effective as IV lorazepam.

? A seizure continues after two adequate doses of IV lorazepam. Which medication is the appropriate next step?
+ An IV loading dose of levetiracetam or fosphenytoin
- A third and fourth dose of lorazepam only | Further benzodiazepines add sedation with little benefit.
- Oral carbamazepine | Oral agents are not used in status.
- IV haloperidol | Antipsychotics lower the seizure threshold.
= Benzodiazepine-refractory status is treated with an IV second-line agent such as levetiracetam, fosphenytoin or valproate.

? IV phenytoin is ordered. Which administration practice is correct?
+ Dilute in normal saline and infuse no faster than 50 mg per minute with cardiac monitoring
- Mix with dextrose 5% and give by rapid push | It precipitates in dextrose and rapid infusion causes hypotension and dysrhythmia.
- Give intramuscularly in the deltoid | Intramuscular phenytoin causes tissue necrosis.
- Infuse it undiluted through a small vein in the back of the hand, without an in-line filter | Extravasation causes severe tissue injury.
= Phenytoin is compatible only with saline and is given slowly through a large vein because of cardiovascular toxicity and tissue injury.

? A patient with diabetes is found convulsing. Which reversible cause should be checked immediately?
+ Hypoglycemia
- Hyperlipidemia | Lipids do not cause acute seizures.
- Hypothyroidism | It is not an immediate reversible cause of seizure.
- Iron deficiency | It does not cause seizures.
= Low blood glucose is a common, rapidly reversible cause of seizures and is checked in every seizing patient.

? A man who drinks heavily stopped two days ago and has now had two generalized seizures. Which medication is most appropriate?
+ A benzodiazepine
- Phenytoin | Phenytoin is not effective for alcohol withdrawal seizures.
- Haloperidol | It lowers the seizure threshold.
- A beta blocker alone | It masks signs without preventing seizures.
= Alcohol withdrawal seizures arise from loss of GABA activity and are treated with benzodiazepines.

? A 2-year-old had a generalized seizure lasting three minutes with a temperature of 39.6 C. He is now alert and playful, with an ear infection found on examination. What should the nurse tell the parents?
+ Simple febrile seizures are common and do not cause brain damage
- He will need anticonvulsants for life | Long-term treatment is not indicated.
- He has epilepsy | A simple febrile seizure is not epilepsy.
- Regular antipyretics at the first sign of fever will prevent another | Antipyretics do not prevent recurrence.
= A simple febrile seizure is generalized, lasts under 15 minutes and does not recur within 24 hours; the prognosis is excellent.

? After a seizure a patient is drowsy and cannot move his left arm. The weakness resolves over two hours. What is this called?
+ Todd's paralysis
- Bell's palsy | That is facial nerve weakness.
- A completed stroke | A stroke deficit would not resolve this quickly in this context.
- Malingering | The finding is physiologic.
= Transient focal weakness after a seizure reflects neuronal exhaustion and resolves within hours, though stroke must be considered.

? A patient's convulsions stopped after benzodiazepines and a second-line drug, but 40 minutes later he is still unresponsive with subtle eyelid twitching. What should the nurse suspect?
+ Ongoing non-convulsive seizure activity
- Normal postictal sleep after a long seizure | The postictal state should be improving by now.
- Oversedation only | Twitching suggests electrical seizure activity.
- A psychiatric disorder | That is unlikely in this setting.
= Failure to regain consciousness after convulsive status raises concern for non-convulsive status, which requires an EEG.

? A patient with known epilepsy has a breakthrough seizure. Which cause is most common?
+ Missed doses of the anticonvulsant
- A new brain tumour | That is far less common than non-adherence.
- Eating a high-protein meal | Diet of this kind does not trigger seizures.
- Mild exercise | Mild exertion is not a common trigger.
= Sub-therapeutic drug levels from missed doses are the leading cause of breakthrough seizures, so a level is checked.

? Which items should be at the bedside of a patient placed on seizure precautions?
+ Suction, oxygen and padded side rails
- A tongue blade and soft wrist restraints | Neither is used during a seizure.
- A meal tray and a water jug | Oral intake is not a safety measure.
- A bedside commode only | That does not address airway or injury.
= Seizure precautions prepare for airway management and injury prevention.
