# Domain 11 — Head, Eye, Ear, Nose & Throat Emergencies (topics 3-5)

@ 11.3
? A 70-year-old with atrial fibrillation suddenly loses all vision in the right eye. There is no pain or redness. How should the triage nurse treat this presentation?
+ As a time-critical emergency equivalent to a stroke
- As a routine eye complaint for a clinic referral | Retinal ischemia causes permanent loss within hours.
- As conjunctivitis | Conjunctivitis does not cause sudden blindness.
- As a migraine | Migraine aura is transient and usually affects both visual fields.
= Sudden painless loss of vision in one eye suggests central retinal artery occlusion, an ocular stroke that needs immediate evaluation.

? A patient has a severely painful red eye, blurred vision with halos around lights, nausea and a pupil that is mid-dilated and does not react. The eye feels very hard. What is the most likely diagnosis?
+ Acute angle-closure glaucoma
- Conjunctivitis | Conjunctivitis does not cause a fixed pupil or severe pain with halos.
- Corneal abrasion | An abrasion does not harden the globe or fix the pupil.
- Retinal detachment | Detachment is painless.
= Sudden blockage of aqueous drainage sharply raises pressure in the eye, causing pain, halos, a cloudy cornea and a fixed mid-dilated pupil.

? Which treatment should the nurse anticipate for acute angle-closure glaucoma?
+ Pressure-lowering eye drops and IV acetazolamide
- Dilating drops | Dilation worsens the angle closure.
- An eye patch and discharge | Untreated high pressure destroys the optic nerve.
- Antibiotic ointment | The condition is not infectious.
= Topical and systemic agents lower the pressure urgently until laser iridotomy can be performed.

? A patient reports flashes of light and a shower of new floaters, followed by a dark curtain moving across the vision of one eye. There is no pain. What should the nurse suspect?
+ Retinal detachment
- Angle-closure glaucoma | That is painful with a red eye.
- Cataract | Cataracts develop gradually.
- Corneal ulcer | An ulcer is painful with a red eye.
= Flashes, floaters and a curtain-like field loss indicate the retina is separating and needs urgent ophthalmology review.

? A 28-year-old woman has lost vision in one eye over two days. Moving the eye is painful, and colours look washed out. Which condition is most likely?
+ Optic neuritis
- Retinal detachment | Detachment is painless.
- Central retinal artery occlusion | That is sudden and painless.
- Conjunctivitis | It does not cause vision loss or pain on movement.
= Painful eye movement with reduced colour vision in a young adult suggests optic neuritis, which is associated with multiple sclerosis.

? A patient cannot read any line on the eye chart, even the largest letter. What should the nurse test next?
+ Ability to count fingers, then to see hand movement, then to perceive light
- Nothing further; record "blind" | Lesser degrees of vision must be documented.
- Colour vision only | Acuity is graded before colour.
- Pupil size only | That does not grade vision.
= Visual acuity is documented in descending steps: counting fingers, hand motion, light perception and no light perception.

? A patient describes a shade coming down over one eye for about five minutes, after which vision returned to normal. What should the nurse recognize?
+ This is a warning sign of stroke that needs urgent evaluation
- This is eye strain | Transient monocular blindness is vascular.
- This needs only an optician's appointment | A stroke may follow within days.
- This is a normal effect of ageing | It is not.
= Amaurosis fugax is a transient ischemic attack of the retina, often from carotid disease.

? A patient known to have narrow drainage angles is wheezing. Which order should the nurse clarify before giving?
+ Nebulized ipratropium by a loosely fitting mask
- Supplemental oxygen | Oxygen does not affect eye pressure.
- Oral prednisone | A short course is not the immediate concern.
- A chest radiograph | It does not affect the eyes.
= Anticholinergic mist reaching the eyes dilates the pupils and can precipitate angle closure; a mouthpiece or well-fitted mask reduces exposure.

? Four days after cataract surgery, a patient has increasing eye pain, falling vision and a layer of white material in the front of the eye. What does this indicate?
+ Infection inside the eye requiring emergency ophthalmology
- Normal postoperative healing | Pain and vision loss are not expected.
- A reaction to the eye shield | A shield does not cause these findings.
- Dry eye | Dry eye does not cause vision loss with a white fluid level.
= Postoperative endophthalmitis threatens the eye and needs immediate intraocular antibiotics.

? Which patient with a red eye needs the most urgent ophthalmology evaluation?
+ A patient with eye pain, reduced vision and sensitivity to light
- A patient with itching and watery discharge in both eyes | That suggests allergic conjunctivitis.
- A patient with a gritty feeling and morning crusting | That suggests bacterial conjunctivitis.
- A patient with a painless bright red patch on the white of the eye | A subconjunctival hemorrhage is benign.
= Pain, vision loss and photophobia are red flags for keratitis, iritis or glaucoma rather than simple conjunctivitis.

@ 11.4
? A 19-year-old has a severe sore throat on one side, a muffled voice, difficulty opening the mouth and a uvula pushed to the opposite side. What is the most likely diagnosis?
+ Peritonsillar abscess
- Viral pharyngitis | It does not displace the uvula or cause trismus.
- Epiglottitis | The oropharynx looks normal in epiglottitis.
- Strep throat | It causes exudate without uvular deviation.
= A collection of pus beside the tonsil causes unilateral pain, a "hot potato" voice, trismus and deviation of the uvula.

? Which treatment should the nurse anticipate for a peritonsillar abscess?
+ Needle aspiration or incision and drainage with antibiotics
- Antihistamines and rest | They do not treat an abscess.
- A throat lozenge and discharge | Pus must be drained.
- Emergency tracheostomy in every case | Most are drained without a surgical airway.
= Drainage relieves symptoms and prevents spread; antibiotics and hydration follow.

? A 45-year-old has a sore throat far worse than the appearance of his throat would suggest, with painful swallowing, a muffled voice and drooling. He prefers to sit leaning forward. What should the nurse suspect?
+ Epiglottitis
- Tonsillitis | The throat would look inflamed.
- A common cold | A cold does not cause drooling and a muffled voice.
- Reflux | Reflux does not cause this presentation.
= Severe throat pain with a near-normal oropharynx, drooling and a tripod posture suggests supraglottic swelling.

? How should the nurse care for an adult with suspected epiglottitis while awaiting the airway team?
+ Keep him sitting upright with airway equipment at the bedside
- Lay him flat for a throat swab | Lying flat and manipulation can close the airway.
- Send him to radiology unaccompanied | He must not leave monitored care.
- Give oral fluids to soothe the throat | He cannot swallow safely.
= The patient stays in the position of comfort, is disturbed as little as possible and is never left without airway support.

? A patient with poor dentition has firm swelling under both sides of the jaw, a tongue pushed upward and a hard, tender floor of the mouth. What is this condition?
+ Ludwig's angina
- Mumps | Mumps swells the parotid glands in front of the ears.
- Peritonsillar abscess | That is unilateral and does not raise the tongue.
- An allergic reaction | Allergy does not cause a hard, tender floor of the mouth with fever.
= Ludwig's angina is a rapidly spreading cellulitis of the submandibular space, usually from a dental infection.

? What is the greatest immediate threat in Ludwig's angina?
+ Airway obstruction
- Tooth loss | It is not life threatening.
- Hearing loss | It is not associated.
- Facial scarring | It is not the immediate danger.
= Swelling displaces the tongue upward and backward; the airway is secured early, and the patient is kept upright.

? A 3-year-old has a fever, drools and holds his neck stiffly, refusing to look up. His voice is muffled. Which condition should the nurse suspect?
+ Retropharyngeal abscess
- Teething | Teething does not cause neck stiffness with fever and a muffled voice.
- Otitis media | It does not cause drooling or neck stiffness.
- Croup | Croup causes a barking cough and stridor.
= A deep neck abscess behind the pharynx causes fever, drooling and refusal to extend the neck in young children.

? A 9-year-old has fever, a sore throat with white patches on the tonsils and tender glands at the front of the neck. He has no cough. Why is antibiotic treatment important if a strep test is positive?
+ To prevent rheumatic fever
- To prevent the common cold | Antibiotics do not prevent viral illness.
- To prevent asthma | There is no link.
- To prevent tooth decay | That is not the purpose.
= Treating streptococcal pharyngitis prevents rheumatic fever and suppurative complications.

? A 17-year-old has a sore throat, extreme fatigue, swollen glands at the back of the neck and a positive mononucleosis test. Which discharge instruction is most important?
+ Avoid contact sports for at least three weeks
- Return to football practice tomorrow | The enlarged spleen can rupture with minor trauma.
- Take amoxicillin for ten days | Amoxicillin causes a rash in mononucleosis and does not treat it.
- Stay in bed for a month | Prolonged bed rest is unnecessary.
= The spleen is enlarged and fragile; contact and strenuous activity are avoided until cleared.

? A child who had a tonsillectomy seven days ago is swallowing frequently and spits bright red blood. What should the nurse do?
+ Treat it as an emergency: keep the child upright, obtain IV access and notify ENT
- Reassure the parents that a little blood is normal | Bleeding a week after surgery can become massive.
- Give ice chips and discharge | The child needs urgent assessment.
- Ask the child to gargle with salt water | Gargling can dislodge clot.
= Secondary hemorrhage occurs 5-10 days after tonsillectomy when the scab separates; frequent swallowing is an early sign.

@ 11.5
? A patient is distressed by a buzzing, moving sensation in the ear. A live cockroach is seen in the canal. What should be done before removal?
+ Kill the insect by filling the canal with mineral oil or lidocaine
- Shine a light to draw it out | Insects often move deeper.
- Pull it out alive with forceps | A struggling insect damages the canal and eardrum.
- Irrigate with cold water | Cold water causes vertigo and does not kill the insect quickly.
= Immobilizing the insect first prevents further injury and pain during removal.

? A child has pushed a dried bean into his ear canal. Which removal method should be avoided?
+ Irrigation with water
- Removal with a small hook under direct vision | That is an acceptable method.
- Removal with suction | That is an acceptable method.
- Referral to ENT if it cannot be grasped | That is appropriate.
= Vegetable matter absorbs water and swells, wedging the object more tightly.

? A toddler has a button battery lodged in one nostril. How urgent is removal?
+ Emergent, because tissue damage begins within hours
- Routine, at a clinic visit next week | The battery causes burns and septal perforation.
- Not needed; it will fall out | It will not, and damage is progressive.
- Only if the child develops a fever | Waiting causes permanent injury.
= Button batteries cause liquefaction necrosis from electrical current and leakage and are removed immediately without irrigation.

? A 3-year-old has had foul-smelling, pus-like discharge from one nostril for a week. What is the most likely cause?
+ A foreign body in the nose
- Seasonal allergy | Allergy causes clear discharge from both nostrils.
- A common cold | A cold affects both nostrils without a foul smell.
- A broken nose | There is no history of trauma.
= One-sided purulent nasal discharge in a young child means a retained foreign body until proven otherwise.

? Which technique can remove a smooth nasal foreign body from a cooperative young child?
+ The parent blows into the child's mouth while the clear nostril is held closed
- Pushing the object backward with a swab | That risks aspiration.
- Asking the child to sniff hard | Sniffing draws the object deeper.
- Irrigating the nostril with a syringe | Fluid can be aspirated.
= Positive pressure from the "parent's kiss" often expels the object without instruments.

? A child has placed a small magnet in each nostril, and they have attached across the septum. Why is this urgent?
+ The magnets compress the septum and cause necrosis and perforation
- The magnets will affect the child's heart | They do not.
- The magnets are poisonous | The danger is mechanical.
- The magnets will dissolve | They will not.
= Paired magnets across the septum cut off the blood supply to the tissue between them.

? A provider has made two unsuccessful attempts to remove a hard bead from a crying child's ear, and the canal is now bleeding. What is the best course?
+ Stop and refer to ENT
- Keep trying with larger forceps | Repeated attempts cause swelling and can perforate the eardrum.
- Irrigate forcefully | A traumatized canal should not be irrigated.
- Send the child home with the bead in place and no follow-up | The object still needs removal.
= Repeated attempts cause edema and injury; removal under magnification or sedation is safer.

? A patient with ear tubes asks to have earwax flushed out. What should the nurse do?
+ Decline irrigation, because the eardrum is not intact
- Irrigate with cold water | Irrigation is contraindicated, and cold water causes vertigo.
- Irrigate with hydrogen peroxide | Fluid would enter the middle ear.
- Irrigate with extra pressure | Pressure adds further harm.
= Irrigation is contraindicated with a perforation or tubes; when it is used, the water is at body temperature to avoid vertigo.

? After a bead is removed from a child's right nostril, what else should the nurse check?
+ The other nostril and both ears
- The child's eyesight | It is unrelated.
- The child's reflexes | They are unrelated.
- Nothing further | Children often place more than one object.
= Young children frequently insert several objects, so all accessible openings are inspected.

? While cleaning her ear with a cotton swab, a woman was bumped and felt sudden sharp pain. She now has reduced hearing and a little blood in the canal. What should the nurse teach?
+ Keep the ear dry; most perforations heal on their own
- Flush the ear with water to clear the blood | Water in the middle ear causes infection.
- Use over-the-counter drops that dry out swimmer's ear | Those drops are painful and unsafe with a perforation.
- Pack the canal tightly with cotton | Packing traps moisture and debris.
= A traumatic eardrum perforation usually heals within weeks if kept dry; vertigo or major hearing loss needs prompt ENT review.
