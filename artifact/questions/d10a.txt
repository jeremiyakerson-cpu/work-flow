# Domain 10 — Medical Emergencies (topics 0-2)

@ 10.0
? A 70-year-old with a cough and fever has RR 26, new confusion and BP 96/58. What should the triage nurse recognize?
+ Probable sepsis requiring immediate evaluation
- A routine respiratory infection suitable for the waiting room | Altered mentation, tachypnea and low pressure indicate organ dysfunction.
- Anxiety from being unwell | These signs have a physiologic cause.
- Dehydration that needs only oral fluids | The picture is more serious than simple dehydration.
= Infection with altered mental status, a fast respiratory rate and low blood pressure suggests sepsis and a high risk of deterioration.

? Which set of interventions makes up the initial sepsis bundle for a patient with septic shock?
+ Lactate, blood cultures, broad-spectrum antibiotics and a crystalloid bolus
- Chest radiograph, urine culture, oral antibiotics and reassessment in the morning | These omit lactate, IV antibiotics and fluid resuscitation.
- Antipyretics, bed rest and observation | These do not treat shock.
- Steroids, diuretics and fluid restriction | These are not initial sepsis therapy.
= The bundle is: measure lactate, draw cultures before antibiotics, give broad-spectrum antibiotics, give fluids for hypotension or high lactate, and add vasopressors if needed.

? An 80 kg patient with sepsis has BP 82/46 and a lactate of 4.8 mmol/L. Using 30 mL/kg, what crystalloid volume should the nurse prepare?
+ 2,400 mL
- 800 mL | That is 10 mL/kg.
- 1,600 mL | That is 20 mL/kg.
- 4,800 mL | That is 60 mL/kg.
= Sepsis-induced hypotension or a lactate of 4 mmol/L or more is treated with 30 mL/kg of crystalloid, here 2,400 mL.
! The 2026 Surviving Sepsis Campaign guideline keeps at least 30 mL/kg in the first 3 hours as a suggestion with low-certainty evidence (SCCM summary; full text not read).

? A patient with septic shock remains hypotensive after the fluid bolus. Which vasopressor is first-line?
+ Norepinephrine
- Dopamine | It causes more dysrhythmias and is no longer first-line.
- Phenylephrine | It is not the first choice in septic shock.
- Dobutamine | It is an inotrope, used for cardiac dysfunction rather than as the first vasopressor.
= Norepinephrine is the recommended first vasopressor for septic shock.
! Norepinephrine as first-line vasopressor was not re-confirmed against the 2026 Surviving Sepsis text.

? What is the initial mean arterial pressure target for a patient with septic shock on vasopressors?
+ 65 mmHg
- 45 mmHg | That is too low to perfuse organs.
- 90 mmHg | A higher routine target has not improved outcomes.
- 110 mmHg | That exposes the patient to excess vasopressor.
= A mean arterial pressure of at least 65 mmHg is the standard initial target.
! The 2026 Surviving Sepsis Campaign guideline keeps 65 mmHg and suggests a 60-65 mmHg range for patients aged 65 and over (SCCM summary).

? A septic patient's first lactate was 5.2 mmol/L. After fluids, what should the nurse anticipate?
+ A repeat lactate within a few hours
- No further lactate testing | A raised value must be rechecked.
- A lactate once daily | That is too infrequent to guide resuscitation.
- Replacing lactate with a troponin | Troponin does not track perfusion.
= An elevated lactate is remeasured to confirm that perfusion is improving with treatment.

? In a patient with septic shock, how soon should broad-spectrum antibiotics be given?
+ Within one hour of recognition
- Within 24 hours | Each hour of delay increases mortality.
- After culture results are back | Results take days.
- After the patient is transferred to intensive care | Antibiotics are started in the ED.
= In septic shock, antibiotics are given immediately, ideally within one hour, after cultures if these cause no delay.

? After 30 mL/kg of fluid, a patient needs norepinephrine to keep the mean arterial pressure at 65 mmHg, and the lactate is 3.4 mmol/L. How is this classified?
+ Septic shock
- Sepsis without shock | A vasopressor requirement with raised lactate after fluids defines shock.
- Simple infection | There is clear organ dysfunction.
- Hypovolemia only | Adequate fluid has been given.
= Septic shock is sepsis with persistent hypotension needing vasopressors and a lactate above 2 mmol/L despite adequate fluid.

? A patient with septic shock needs rising doses of norepinephrine. Which additional therapy may be ordered?
+ Vasopressin, and IV hydrocortisone for refractory shock
- A beta blocker | It would lower blood pressure further.
- A loop diuretic | Volume removal worsens shock.
- Stopping all fluids and antibiotics to rest the circulation | Treatment must continue.
= Vasopressin is added to norepinephrine when the dose is escalating, and low-dose hydrocortisone is used for shock that persists.

? Norepinephrine is running through a forearm IV. The site becomes pale, cool and swollen. What should the nurse do?
+ Stop the infusion at that site and prepare phentolamine for infiltration
- Slow the infusion a little and continue at the same site while it is watched | Continued infusion causes tissue necrosis.
- Apply a warm pack and continue | Heat does not reverse vasoconstriction from the drug.
- Flush the line forcefully with saline | Flushing pushes more drug into the tissue.
= Extravasated vasopressor causes ischemic necrosis; the infusion is moved and the area treated with phentolamine.

? A patient with septic shock has a large perirectal abscess. Besides antibiotics and fluids, what is essential?
+ Prompt drainage of the abscess
- Waiting to see whether antibiotics shrink it | Antibiotics penetrate abscesses poorly.
- Applying a dressing only | A dressing does not remove the source.
- Delaying any procedure until shock resolves | The shock will not resolve while the source remains.
= Source control, such as draining pus or removing infected devices, is a core part of sepsis treatment.

@ 10.1
? An 18-year-old with type 1 diabetes has deep, rapid breathing, a fruity odour on the breath, abdominal pain and a glucose of 486 mg/dL. Which acid-base disorder is present?
+ Metabolic acidosis with an elevated anion gap
- Respiratory acidosis | The breathing is compensatory hyperventilation.
- Metabolic alkalosis | Ketoacids lower the pH.
- Respiratory alkalosis alone from hyperventilation | The primary problem is metabolic.
= Ketoacids cause an anion-gap metabolic acidosis; Kussmaul breathing is the respiratory compensation.

? Which treatment comes first in diabetic ketoacidosis?
+ IV isotonic fluid
- An insulin bolus | Insulin before fluids can cause vascular collapse and hypokalemia.
- Sodium bicarbonate | It is reserved for extreme acidosis.
- Oral glucose | The patient is already hyperglycemic.
= Patients are profoundly volume depleted; fluid restores perfusion and lowers glucose even before insulin starts.

? A patient in diabetic ketoacidosis has a potassium of 3.0 mEq/L. What must happen before insulin is started?
+ Potassium replacement
- Nothing; insulin starts at once | Insulin drives potassium into cells and can cause fatal hypokalemia.
- A dose of sodium polystyrene | That would lower potassium further.
- A loop diuretic | That would lower potassium further.
= Insulin drives potassium into cells, and total body potassium is already depleted; insulin is withheld until potassium has been replaced to at least 3.5 mEq/L.
! The 2024 ADA consensus uses 3.5 mEq/L as the threshold; older references use 3.3. Checked against secondary summaries only.

? A patient in diabetic ketoacidosis has a glucose that has fallen to 210 mg/dL, but the anion gap is still elevated. What should the nurse anticipate?
+ Adding dextrose to the IV fluids and continuing the insulin infusion
- Stopping the insulin infusion | Ketosis will recur if insulin stops.
- Switching to oral diabetes tablets and stopping all intravenous therapy | They do not treat ketoacidosis.
- Giving a large bolus of insulin | That causes hypoglycemia.
= Insulin is continued until the acidosis resolves; dextrose prevents hypoglycemia while ketones clear.

? Which finding shows that diabetic ketoacidosis has resolved?
+ A venous pH of 7.3 or higher with ketones cleared
- A glucose below 250 mg/dL as the only measure used | Glucose normalizes well before the acidosis clears.
- The patient asking for something to eat | Appetite is not a resolution criterion.
- Urine that tests negative for glucose | That does not reflect ketosis or acidosis.
= Resolution is defined by clearance of ketones with a venous pH of 7.3 or more (or bicarbonate of 18 mEq/L or more), not by glucose alone.
! 2024 ADA consensus criteria (ketones below 0.6 mmol/L with pH 7.3 or bicarbonate 18); older criteria used closure of the anion gap. Checked against secondary summaries only.

? A 9-year-old being treated for diabetic ketoacidosis develops a headache, becomes drowsy and has a heart rate that has slowed to 58. What should the nurse suspect?
+ Cerebral edema
- Normal sleepiness | These signs are ominous during treatment.
- Hypoglycemia only | Glucose is checked, but bradycardia with headache points to raised intracranial pressure.
- Migraine | That is unlikely in this setting.
= Cerebral edema is the leading cause of death in children with ketoacidosis and is treated with mannitol or hypertonic saline.

? A 78-year-old with type 2 diabetes is lethargic with a glucose of 1,040 mg/dL, very dry mucous membranes and only trace ketones. Which condition is most likely?
+ Hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state
- Diabetic ketoacidosis | Significant ketosis and acidosis would be present.
- Hypoglycemia | The glucose is extremely high.
- Lactic acidosis from metformin | The picture is dominated by hyperglycemia and dehydration.
= Hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state features extreme hyperglycemia and dehydration with little ketosis; fluids are the mainstay.

? A patient with diabetes is shaky and sweaty but awake and able to swallow. Glucose is 52 mg/dL. What should the nurse give?
+ About 15 g of fast-acting carbohydrate by mouth
- IV dextrose 50% | IV treatment is for patients who cannot take oral glucose.
- An intramuscular glucagon injection from the emergency kit | That is for patients who cannot swallow and have no IV access.
- A high-fat snack only | Fat slows glucose absorption.
= A conscious patient is treated with 15 g of rapid carbohydrate, with a recheck in 15 minutes.

? An 82-year-old with kidney disease became hypoglycemic on glyburide and responded to IV dextrose. Why should she be observed rather than discharged?
+ The drug's effect is prolonged and hypoglycemia is likely to recur
- Dextrose causes delayed allergic reactions | That is not the concern.
- She needs teaching about how to give herself insulin injections first | She is not on insulin.
- Hypoglycemia never recurs after treatment | It commonly does with long-acting agents.
= Sulfonylureas, especially with impaired renal clearance, cause recurrent hypoglycemia for many hours.

? A patient taking an SGLT2 inhibitor has vomiting, rapid deep breathing and an anion gap of 26, but the glucose is only 178 mg/dL. What should the nurse recognize?
+ Ketoacidosis can occur with a near-normal glucose on this medication
- A normal glucose excludes ketoacidosis | That is not true with SGLT2 inhibitors.
- The symptoms must be from gastroenteritis, as the glucose is not raised | The anion gap indicates acidosis.
- The patient needs only an antiemetic | She has a metabolic emergency.
= SGLT2 inhibitors can cause euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis, so ketones are checked despite the glucose.

? In diabetic ketoacidosis, when is IV sodium bicarbonate considered?
+ Only for very severe acidosis, with a pH below 7.0
- For every patient on arrival | Routine use offers no benefit and can cause harm.
- Whenever the glucose is above 400 mg/dL on two readings in a row | Glucose level is not the indication.
- Never under any circumstance | It is considered in extreme acidosis.
= Fluids and insulin correct the acidosis; bicarbonate is reserved for extreme cases because it can worsen hypokalemia and cerebral edema.
! The 2024 ADA consensus uses pH below 7.0; older references use 6.9. Checked against secondary summaries only.

@ 10.2
? A woman with untreated Graves' disease develops a temperature of 40.3 C, a heart rate of 164 in atrial fibrillation, agitation and vomiting after a chest infection. What is this condition?
+ Thyroid storm
- Myxedema coma | That presents with hypothermia and bradycardia.
- Neuroleptic malignant syndrome | There is no antipsychotic exposure.
- Heat stroke | There is no heat exposure, and thyroid disease is present.
= Thyroid storm is life-threatening thyrotoxicosis with high fever, marked tachycardia, agitation and gastrointestinal upset.

? Which medication is given to control heart rate and adrenergic symptoms in thyroid storm?
+ Propranolol
- Levothyroxine | It would worsen thyrotoxicosis.
- Epinephrine | It would worsen tachycardia.
- Atropine | It increases heart rate.
= Beta blockade controls the cardiovascular effects, and propranolol also reduces conversion of T4 to T3.

? In thyroid storm, why is iodine given at least an hour after the antithyroid drug?
+ Given first, iodine would be used to make more thyroid hormone
- Iodine inactivates the antithyroid drug | That is not the reason.
- Iodine causes immediate allergic reactions | That is not the reason for the sequence.
- The two drugs cannot be given through the same vein | Compatibility is not the issue.
= The thionamide blocks hormone synthesis first; iodine then blocks hormone release without fuelling production.

? A patient in thyroid storm has a temperature of 40.1 C. Which antipyretic should be avoided?
+ Aspirin
- Acetaminophen | It is the preferred antipyretic.
- A cooling blanket | External cooling is appropriate.
- Cool IV fluids | These are appropriate.
= Aspirin displaces thyroid hormone from binding proteins and raises free hormone levels.

? Which antithyroid drug is often preferred in thyroid storm because it also blocks conversion of T4 to T3?
+ Propylthiouracil
- Levothyroxine | That is thyroid hormone replacement.
- Liothyronine | That is active thyroid hormone.
- Radioactive iodine | It is not used acutely.
= Propylthiouracil blocks both hormone synthesis and peripheral conversion, although it carries a risk of liver toxicity.

? An elderly woman is found unresponsive in an unheated house. Temperature is 32.4 C, HR 42, RR 8, with a puffy face and a thyroidectomy scar. What should the nurse suspect?
+ Myxedema coma
- Thyroid storm | That causes fever and tachycardia.
- Opioid overdose | The scar, puffiness and hypothermia point to hypothyroidism.
- Stroke | That does not explain the hypothermia and bradycardia.
= Severe untreated hypothyroidism causes hypothermia, bradycardia, hypoventilation and coma, often precipitated by cold or infection.

? Which medications should the nurse anticipate for myxedema coma?
+ IV levothyroxine with IV hydrocortisone
- Propylthiouracil with an iodine solution | Those treat an overactive thyroid.
- Propranolol | It would worsen bradycardia.
- Oral levothyroxine alone | Gut absorption is unreliable in this state.
= IV thyroid hormone is given with a corticosteroid, because adrenal insufficiency may coexist and be unmasked.

? How should a hypothermic patient in myxedema coma be rewarmed?
+ Passively, with blankets and a warm room
- With a forced-air warming blanket on high | Rapid external warming causes vasodilation and collapse.
- With immersion in a hot bath | That risks cardiovascular collapse.
- With heating pads on the limbs | Peripheral heating causes vasodilation and burns.
= Passive rewarming avoids the sudden vasodilation and hypotension that active external warming can cause.

? A patient with myxedema coma is restless during assessment. Why should sedatives and opioids be avoided or greatly reduced?
+ Drug metabolism is slowed, and they can cause profound respiratory depression
- They cause hyperthyroidism | They do not affect thyroid hormone production.
- They are ineffective in hypothyroidism, so very large doses would have to be given | They are overly effective.
- They raise body temperature | That is not the concern.
= Hypothyroid patients are very sensitive to central nervous system depressants and are already hypoventilating.

? Which laboratory pattern is expected in primary hypothyroidism with myxedema coma?
+ High TSH with low free T4
- Low TSH with high free T4 | That is the pattern of thyrotoxicosis.
- Normal TSH with normal free T4 | That is a normal result.
- Low TSH with low cortisol only | That does not define primary hypothyroidism.
= A failing thyroid produces little hormone, so the pituitary raises TSH.

? A patient taking methimazole for hyperthyroidism has a sore throat and a temperature of 38.9 C. Which test is urgent?
+ A complete blood count with differential
- A throat culture only, with results in two days | Infection may be present, but the drug's toxicity must be excluded.
- A thyroid ultrasound | It does not address the concern.
- A serum calcium | It is not the priority.
= Antithyroid drugs can cause agranulocytosis; fever or sore throat requires an immediate neutrophil count.
