# Domain 6 — Neurological Emergencies (topics 3-5)

@ 6.3
? An unidentified man is found unresponsive in a park and brought in by ambulance. After the airway and breathing are secured, which assessment comes next?
+ A bedside blood glucose
- A CT scan of the head | Imaging follows rapid reversible checks.
- A toxicology screen | Results are slow and seldom change immediate care.
- A lumbar puncture | It is not an early step in an undifferentiated coma.
= Hypoglycemia is common, quickly identified and immediately treatable, so it is checked first in altered mental status.

? A malnourished man with alcohol use disorder is confused, has an unsteady wide-based gait and cannot move his eyes fully to the side. Which treatment is the priority?
+ IV thiamine
- IV haloperidol | Sedation does not treat the cause.
- A high-carbohydrate meal alone | Glucose without thiamine can worsen the condition.
- IV naloxone | There are no signs of opioid toxicity.
= Confusion, ataxia and ophthalmoplegia are the triad of Wernicke encephalopathy, treated with parenteral thiamine.

? A patient with cirrhosis is drowsy and disoriented, and his hands flap when the wrists are extended. Which laboratory test is most relevant?
+ Serum ammonia
- Serum lipase | It evaluates the pancreas.
- Troponin | It evaluates myocardial injury.
- Thyroid-stimulating hormone | Thyroid disease is not suggested.
= Asterixis with confusion in liver disease suggests hepatic encephalopathy, supported by a raised ammonia level.

? An 86-year-old woman who was independent yesterday is now agitated and disoriented. Her family reports that she started an over-the-counter sleep aid three nights ago. What is the most likely cause?
+ Anticholinergic effects of the sleep aid
- Rapid progression of Alzheimer's disease | Dementia does not appear over days.
- Normal ageing | Acute confusion is never normal.
- Vitamin C deficiency | It does not cause acute delirium.
= Many non-prescription sleep aids contain diphenhydramine, a common cause of delirium in older adults.

? A patient with COPD receiving 6 L/min of oxygen has become progressively sleepy and hard to rouse. SpO2 is 99%. Which test will identify the cause?
+ An arterial or venous blood gas
- A head CT | The clinical picture points to a respiratory cause.
- A serum sodium | It does not explain the picture.
- A urinalysis | It does not explain the picture.
= Somnolence with high-flow oxygen in COPD suggests carbon dioxide narcosis, confirmed by an elevated PaCO2.

? A confused patient has a serum sodium of 112 mEq/L that has developed over several weeks. Why must the sodium be corrected slowly?
+ Rapid correction can cause osmotic demyelination
- Rapid correction causes hyperkalemia | Potassium is not the concern.
- Slow correction prevents infection | Infection is unrelated.
- Sodium cannot be given intravenously | It can, with careful monitoring.
= Raising chronic hyponatremia by more than about 8-10 mEq/L in 24 hours risks irreversible brainstem injury.

? When assessing the motor response of an unresponsive patient, which stimulus is most appropriate?
+ A firm squeeze of the trapezius muscle
- A sternal rub with the knuckles for a minute | Prolonged sternal rubbing causes bruising and is discouraged.
- A pinch of the nipple | That is inappropriate and unacceptable.
- A needle prick to the foot | It can provoke a spinal reflex rather than a cerebral response.
= A central stimulus such as trapezius squeeze tests the brain's response rather than a spinal reflex.

? A patient with a head injury becomes unresponsive, and the right pupil is now fixed and dilated. What does this indicate?
+ Compression of the oculomotor nerve from brain herniation
- An effect of bright lighting | Light constricts the pupils.
- Opioid toxicity | Opioids cause bilateral pinpoint pupils.
- A normal variant | A new change with coma is not a variant.
= A unilateral fixed dilated pupil signals uncal herniation compressing the third cranial nerve, a neurosurgical emergency.

? An unresponsive patient has bilateral pinpoint pupils and slow respirations. Besides opioid overdose, which condition should be considered if naloxone has no effect?
+ Pontine hemorrhage
- Cocaine toxicity | Stimulants dilate the pupils.
- Anticholinergic poisoning | It dilates the pupils.
- Hypothermia alone | It does not typically cause pinpoint pupils.
= A bleed in the pons interrupts sympathetic pathways, producing pinpoint pupils and coma.

? A patient with diabetes is unresponsive with a glucose of 32 mg/dL. No IV access can be obtained. Which treatment should the nurse give?
+ Intramuscular glucagon
- Oral glucose gel | An unresponsive patient cannot swallow safely.
- Subcutaneous insulin | Insulin would lower the glucose further.
- Oral juice through a straw | Aspiration risk is high.
= Glucagon mobilizes liver glycogen and is used when the patient cannot take oral glucose and has no IV access.

? A 58-year-old with no psychiatric history has sudden confusion, visual hallucinations, HR 128 and temperature 38.9 C. A colleague suggests a psychiatric referral. What is the best response?
+ These features point to a medical cause that must be worked up first
- Agree; hallucinations are always psychiatric | Visual hallucinations suggest an organic cause.
- Agree; fever is common with anxiety | Fever is not explained by anxiety.
- Suggest discharge with outpatient counselling | Acute delirium is a medical emergency.
= Late onset, abrupt change, visual hallucinations and abnormal vital signs indicate delirium from a medical cause.

? The mnemonic AEIOU-TIPS is used for which purpose?
+ Recalling the causes of altered mental status
- Scoring stroke severity | That is done with the NIH Stroke Scale.
- Grading pressure injuries | That uses a staging system.
- Calculating burn fluid needs | That uses a resuscitation formula.
= AEIOU-TIPS covers alcohol, epilepsy, insulin, overdose, uremia, trauma, infection, psychiatric causes and stroke or shock.

@ 6.4
? Which change is usually the earliest sign of rising intracranial pressure?
+ A decline in level of consciousness
- A fixed dilated pupil | Pupil changes come later.
- Bradycardia with hypertension | Those are late, pre-terminal signs.
- Decerebrate posturing | Posturing is a late sign.
= The cerebral cortex is most sensitive to falling perfusion, so restlessness, confusion or drowsiness appear first.

? A head-injured patient has BP 196/72, HR 46 and irregular respirations. What do these findings represent?
+ Cushing's triad, indicating impending herniation
- Hypovolemic shock | Shock causes hypotension and tachycardia.
- A vasovagal episode | That is brief and not associated with a widened pulse pressure.
- Neurogenic shock | That causes hypotension with bradycardia.
= Hypertension with a wide pulse pressure, bradycardia and irregular breathing is a late sign of critically raised intracranial pressure.

? How should a patient with raised intracranial pressure be positioned?
+ Head of the bed at 30 degrees with the head in the midline
- Flat with the head turned to one side | Rotation compresses the jugular veins.
- Trendelenburg | Head-down positioning raises intracranial pressure.
- Sitting at 90 degrees with the hips sharply flexed | Hip flexion raises intra-abdominal and intracranial pressure.
= Elevation with neutral alignment promotes venous drainage from the head without compromising perfusion.

? A teenager was struck on the temple by a baseball, lost consciousness briefly, then seemed normal for an hour. He is now drowsy and vomiting. Which injury fits this pattern?
+ Epidural hematoma
- Simple concussion | Concussion does not deteriorate after a lucid interval.
- Chronic subdural hematoma | That develops over weeks.
- Scalp hematoma | A scalp bruise does not cause drowsiness and vomiting.
= Arterial bleeding from the middle meningeal artery classically causes a lucid interval followed by rapid decline.

? An 82-year-old on apixaban fell three weeks ago. She now has a mild headache, increasing forgetfulness and a slight limp. What should the nurse suspect?
+ Chronic subdural hematoma
- Epidural hematoma | Epidural bleeds present within hours.
- Normal ageing | New neurologic change is not normal ageing.
- A viral illness | That does not explain focal weakness.
= Slow venous bleeding from torn bridging veins in older or anticoagulated patients produces subtle symptoms over days to weeks.

? Which finding indicates a basilar skull fracture?
+ Bruising behind the ear with clear fluid draining from the ear canal
- A laceration over the forehead | A scalp wound does not indicate skull base injury.
- A swollen lower lip | That is a facial soft tissue injury.
- Neck pain on rotation | That suggests a cervical injury.
= Battle's sign, periorbital bruising and cerebrospinal fluid leaking from the ear or nose are signs of skull base fracture.

? A patient with a severe head injury develops a dilated pupil and extensor posturing. Which medication should the nurse anticipate?
+ A hyperosmolar agent such as mannitol or hypertonic saline
- IV furosemide alone | A loop diuretic alone is not the treatment for herniation.
- IV dextrose 5% in water | Hypotonic fluid worsens cerebral edema.
- IV nitroprusside | Vasodilators raise intracranial pressure.
= Hyperosmolar therapy draws water out of brain tissue and is used for signs of herniation.

? A ventilated patient with a head injury has no signs of herniation. Which carbon dioxide target is appropriate?
+ Normal, about 35 to 40 mmHg
- 20 to 25 mmHg | Aggressive hyperventilation causes cerebral ischemia.
- 50 to 55 mmHg | Hypercapnia dilates cerebral vessels and raises pressure.
- No target is needed | Carbon dioxide strongly affects cerebral blood flow.
= Normocapnia is maintained; brief mild hyperventilation is reserved for active herniation as a bridge to definitive care.

? A patient with a traumatic brain injury has a mean arterial pressure of 90 mmHg and an intracranial pressure of 25 mmHg. What is the cerebral perfusion pressure?
+ 65 mmHg
- 115 mmHg | That adds the two values.
- 25 mmHg | That is the intracranial pressure itself.
- 3.6 mmHg | That divides rather than subtracts.
= Cerebral perfusion pressure equals mean arterial pressure minus intracranial pressure; a target of about 60-70 mmHg is usual.

? During resuscitation of a patient with a severe head injury, which finding does the most to worsen outcome?
+ A systolic pressure of 82 mmHg
- A temperature of 37.2 C | That is normal.
- A heart rate of 88 | That is normal.
- A glucose of 128 mg/dL | That is acceptable.
= Even one episode of hypotension or hypoxia markedly increases mortality after traumatic brain injury.

? A 16-year-old is discharged after a concussion. Which instruction is most important for his parents?
+ Return at once for worsening headache, repeated vomiting, confusion or unusual drowsiness
- Keep him awake for 24 hours | Sleep is allowed; he should be easy to rouse.
- He may return to football practice tomorrow | Return to play follows a stepwise protocol after symptoms resolve.
- Give aspirin for the headache | Aspirin increases bleeding risk.
= Warning signs of an expanding intracranial bleed can develop after discharge and need immediate re-evaluation.

? A patient responds to painful stimulation by extending and internally rotating both arms with the legs rigidly extended. How should the nurse interpret this?
+ Decerebrate posturing, indicating brainstem dysfunction
- Decorticate posturing, a less severe finding | Decorticate posturing involves flexion of the arms.
- A purposeful withdrawal response | Withdrawal is a normal flexion away from the stimulus.
- A seizure | The response is stimulus driven, not rhythmic.
= Extensor posturing reflects injury at the level of the brainstem and carries a worse prognosis than flexor posturing.

@ 6.5
? A 20-year-old college student has fever, severe headache, a stiff neck and discomfort in bright light. What should the nurse suspect?
+ Bacterial meningitis
- Migraine | Migraine does not cause fever with neck rigidity.
- Influenza | Influenza does not cause marked nuchal rigidity.
- Tension headache | That does not cause fever or photophobia with a stiff neck.
= Fever, headache, neck stiffness and photophobia in a young adult in shared housing suggest bacterial meningitis.

? A patient with suspected bacterial meningitis needs a head CT before lumbar puncture, and the scanner is occupied for 40 minutes. What should happen in the meantime?
+ Draw blood cultures and give antibiotics now
- Hold antibiotics until the spinal fluid is collected | Delay in antibiotics increases mortality.
- Hold antibiotics until the CT is reported | Imaging must not delay treatment.
- Give only an antipyretic | Fever control does not treat the infection.
= Antibiotics are given as soon as bacterial meningitis is suspected, after blood cultures if these cause no delay.

? Which medication is given just before or with the first antibiotic dose in adults with suspected bacterial meningitis?
+ Dexamethasone
- Furosemide | It has no role in meningitis.
- Phenytoin | It is not given routinely.
- Heparin | Anticoagulation is not indicated.
= Dexamethasone reduces inflammation and lowers the risk of hearing loss and death in pneumococcal meningitis.

? Which isolation precaution is needed for a patient with suspected meningococcal meningitis?
+ Droplet precautions until 24 hours of effective antibiotics
- Airborne precautions in a negative-pressure room for the entire stay | The organism spreads by droplets, not by the airborne route.
- Contact precautions only | Contact precautions do not address respiratory droplets.
- Standard precautions only | Close contact carries real transmission risk.
= Neisseria meningitidis spreads by respiratory droplets; a surgical mask and droplet precautions are used until 24 hours of treatment.

? A febrile, lethargic teenager has a rash of purple spots on the trunk and legs that does not blanch when pressed. What does this indicate?
+ Meningococcal septicemia requiring immediate treatment
- A viral rash that needs no action | Non-blanching purpura with fever is an emergency.
- An allergic reaction to food | Allergic rashes blanch and itch.
- Heat rash | Heat rash is fine, blanching and benign.
= A non-blanching petechial or purpuric rash with fever suggests meningococcemia, which can kill within hours.

? A nurse intubated a patient later confirmed to have meningococcal meningitis and was not wearing a mask. What does she need?
+ Antibiotic prophylaxis
- No action, since she feels well | Exposure to respiratory secretions carries a real risk.
- A meningococcal vaccine only | Vaccine does not protect soon enough.
- A two-week quarantine without medication | Prophylaxis, not quarantine, is indicated.
= Close contacts and staff with unprotected exposure to respiratory secretions receive prophylaxis such as ciprofloxacin, rifampin or ceftriaxone.

? Which cerebrospinal fluid result is most consistent with bacterial meningitis?
+ Cloudy fluid with high neutrophils, high protein and low glucose
- Clear fluid with normal protein and glucose | That is a normal result.
- Clear fluid with lymphocytes and normal glucose | That pattern suggests viral meningitis.
- Bloody fluid that clears in successive tubes | That suggests a traumatic tap.
= Bacteria consume glucose and provoke a neutrophil response, making the fluid turbid with high protein.

? Which patient needs a head CT before lumbar puncture?
+ A patient with papilledema and a new facial droop
- A patient who is alert with headache and fever only | No signs of raised pressure or a mass are present.
- A patient with a stiff neck and photophobia only | These are meningeal signs, not signs of a mass.
- A healthy patient with a normal neurologic examination | No indication for imaging first.
= Signs of raised intracranial pressure or a focal lesion, reduced consciousness, seizures and immunocompromise call for CT first.

? Which presentation of meningitis is typical in a 4-month-old?
+ Poor feeding, irritability and a bulging fontanelle
- Complaints of a stiff neck | Infants cannot report this and often lack nuchal rigidity.
- A classic positive Kernig sign | Meningeal signs are unreliable in infants.
- Photophobia described by the child | An infant cannot describe it.
= Infants show non-specific signs: fever or hypothermia, irritability, lethargy, poor feeding, a high-pitched cry and a tense fontanelle.

? A patient has fever, bizarre behaviour, new seizures and temporal lobe changes on imaging. Which medication should the nurse anticipate?
+ IV acyclovir
- IV vancomycin alone | Antibacterial therapy does not treat a viral encephalitis.
- Oral oseltamivir | That treats influenza.
- IV fluconazole | That treats fungal infection.
= Herpes simplex encephalitis targets the temporal lobes; acyclovir is started on suspicion, with hydration to protect the kidneys.

? With the patient supine, the nurse flexes the neck and the hips and knees flex involuntarily. What is this finding?
+ A positive Brudzinski sign
- A positive Babinski sign | That is upgoing toes on plantar stimulation.
- A positive Kernig sign | That is pain on extending the knee with the hip flexed.
- A positive Homans sign | That is calf pain on dorsiflexion.
= Brudzinski's sign indicates meningeal irritation: neck flexion provokes reflex flexion of the hips and knees.

? A patient develops a headache the day after a lumbar puncture that is severe when she sits up and eases when she lies flat. What should the nurse recommend first?
+ Lying flat with oral fluids and caffeine
- Sitting upright as much as possible | Upright posture worsens the headache.
- Fluid restriction | Restriction worsens low cerebrospinal fluid pressure.
- Vigorous exercise | Exertion aggravates the leak.
= A post-dural puncture headache is positional; conservative measures come first, with an epidural blood patch if it persists.
