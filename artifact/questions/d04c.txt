# Domain 4 — Professional Issues (topics 6-8)

@ 4.6
? A man waiting for results begins pacing, clenching his fists and muttering loudly about the delay. What should the nurse do?
+ Approach calmly at a safe distance, acknowledge his frustration and update him
- Ignore him so the behaviour is not rewarded | Early warning signs that are ignored tend to escalate.
- Tell him firmly to sit down or leave | Commands and threats provoke agitated people.
- Stand close and place a hand on his shoulder | Touch and crowding feel threatening.
= Pacing, clenched fists and a raised voice are early signs of escalation; early verbal de-escalation is most effective.

? Which technique is most effective when de-escalating an agitated patient?
+ Speak in a calm, low voice and offer simple choices
- Match the patient's volume to show authority | Raising the voice escalates the encounter.
- Explain in detail why the patient is wrong | Arguing increases agitation.
- Stand squarely in front with arms crossed | A confrontational stance signals threat.
= A calm tone, respect for personal space, active listening and realistic choices give the patient a sense of control.

? Where should the nurse stand when entering the room of a potentially violent patient?
+ Near the door, with an unobstructed path to the exit
- At the far side of the bed, away from the door | The nurse could be trapped.
- Directly in the doorway, blocking it completely | A cornered patient is more likely to attack.
- Seated at the bedside within arm's reach | Being seated and close limits the ability to retreat.
= Staff keep their own exit clear without making the patient feel trapped.

? A visitor in the waiting room lifts his jacket to show a handgun and threatens staff. What is the nurse's best action?
+ Move away from him and activate security and police
- Try to take the weapon from him | Disarming attempts by untrained staff cause injuries.
- Stay and argue him out of it | Remaining nearby increases the danger.
- Ask other visitors to restrain him | Bystanders must not be put at risk.
= With a weapon present, staff remove themselves and others from danger and summon trained responders.

? A nurse is punched in the arm by an intoxicated patient. A colleague says, "That's just part of working in the ED." What is the correct response?
+ Assault is not part of the job and must be reported
- Reporting is needed only if a bone is broken | Every assault is reportable.
- Reporting would get the patient in trouble, so it is unkind | Reporting protects staff and guides prevention.
- It only counts if the patient intended it | Intent does not change the duty to report.
= Under-reporting hides the scale of workplace violence; each incident is reported and the injured nurse is evaluated.

? Which situation carries the highest risk of patient violence in the ED?
+ An intoxicated patient with a history of assault who has waited four hours
- A sleeping child with a fever | The child poses no risk of violence.
- An elderly woman awaiting transport home | She has no risk factors for violence.
- A patient having a wrist radiograph | There are no risk factors described.
= Substance intoxication, previous violence and long waits are leading predictors of violence.

? An alert announces an active shooter in the building. Evacuation routes are blocked. What should staff do?
+ Hide in a locked or barricaded room, silence phones and stay quiet
- Walk toward the sound to assess the situation | Moving toward the threat endangers staff.
- Gather in the open nurses' station | An open area offers no protection.
- Pull the fire alarm | A fire alarm draws people into corridors.
= The sequence is run if a safe route exists, hide if it does not, and fight only as a last resort.

? Which item should a nurse avoid wearing when caring for a combative patient?
+ A stethoscope draped around the neck
- Closed-toe shoes | Sturdy shoes are protective.
- A breakaway identification lanyard | A breakaway lanyard releases if grabbed.
- A watch with a second hand | A watch poses little risk.
= Items around the neck can be used to choke; they are removed or replaced with breakaway versions.

? A family member shouts, "If someone doesn't see my mother now, somebody is going to get hurt!" What is the best first response?
+ State calmly that threats are not acceptable and explain what will happen next for his mother
- Tell him he will be arrested immediately | Counter-threats escalate the situation.
- Move his mother ahead of sicker patients | Rewarding threats undermines triage and safety.
- Walk away without responding | Leaving him without information increases anger.
= Clear limits combined with empathy and information address both the behaviour and the concern behind it.

? After a violent incident in which a patient was restrained, what should the charge nurse arrange for staff?
+ A debriefing with access to support resources
- Nothing, as the event is over | Unaddressed events lead to distress and burnout.
- A written reprimand for those involved | Punishment discourages reporting.
- An instruction not to discuss the event with anyone | Silence prevents learning and support.
= Post-incident debriefing supports staff, identifies what worked and feeds improvements to prevention.

@ 4.7
? A provider orders a medication dose that is ten times the usual amount. The provider insists it is correct when questioned. What should the nurse do next?
+ Withhold the dose and follow the chain of command
- Give the dose, since the provider confirmed it | The nurse remains accountable for giving an unsafe dose.
- Give half the dose as a compromise | Altering an order without authority is outside the nurse's scope.
- Ask another nurse to give it | That passes the hazard on.
= Nurses have an independent duty to protect the patient and escalate unresolved safety concerns through the chain of command.

? Which task may an emergency nurse delegate to an unlicensed technician?
+ Measuring vital signs on a stable patient awaiting discharge
- Assessing a new patient's chest pain | Assessment requires a nurse's judgment.
- Teaching insulin injection technique | Patient teaching is a nursing responsibility.
- Evaluating the response to a nebulizer | Evaluation requires nursing judgment.
= Routine tasks with predictable outcomes on stable patients may be delegated; assessment, teaching and evaluation may not.

? A nurse delegates a blood glucose check to a technician, who records a value of 42 mg/dL but does not report it. Who is accountable for the patient's outcome?
+ The nurse, who remains responsible for supervision and follow-up
- Only the technician | Delegation transfers the task, not accountability.
- The charge nurse alone | The delegating nurse holds primary accountability.
- No one, as the task was delegated correctly | Supervision and follow-up are part of delegation.
= The delegating nurse retains accountability for the outcome and must communicate expectations and follow up.

? A capable adult Jehovah's Witness with a bleeding ulcer refuses blood transfusion after a full discussion of the risks. Which ethical principle requires the nurse to respect this?
+ Autonomy
- Paternalism | Paternalism would override the patient's choice.
- Justice | Justice concerns fairness in distributing care.
- Fidelity | Fidelity concerns keeping promises.
= Autonomy is the right of a capable person to make decisions about their own body, even when others disagree.

? A nurse double-checks an infusion pump setting to avoid giving an overdose. Which ethical principle does this best illustrate?
+ Nonmaleficence
- Autonomy | Autonomy concerns the patient's own choices.
- Justice | Justice concerns fair distribution of care.
- Veracity | Veracity concerns truthfulness.
= Nonmaleficence is the obligation to avoid causing harm.

? A local celebrity with a minor laceration demands to be seen ahead of others. The department is busy with sicker patients. Which principle guides the nurse's response?
+ Justice
- Beneficence | Beneficence is doing good for the individual, not allocating fairly.
- Fidelity | Fidelity concerns faithfulness to commitments.
- Autonomy | Autonomy does not entitle anyone to priority.
= Justice requires that patients be treated fairly according to clinical need, not status.

? At the start of a shift a nurse notices that a colleague has slurred speech and smells of alcohol. What should the nurse do?
+ Report the observations to the charge nurse or supervisor immediately
- Offer to cover the colleague's patients quietly | Covering up leaves patients and the colleague at risk.
- Confront the colleague in front of patients | Public confrontation is unprofessional and unsafe.
- Wait to see whether an error occurs | Waiting knowingly exposes patients to harm.
= A nurse who suspects impairment has a duty to report it so the colleague is removed from care and can get help.

? Which pattern most strongly suggests that a nurse may be diverting controlled substances?
+ Frequent opioid wastage without a witness and patients reporting unrelieved pain
- Always asking a second nurse to witness waste | That is correct practice.
- Declining overtime shifts | That is not a sign of diversion.
- Documenting pain scores after each dose | That is correct practice.
= Wastage discrepancies, unwitnessed waste and patients whose pain is not relieved despite documented doses are warning signs.

? A nurse posts on social media about "the wildest trauma case tonight," giving the injury, the time and the town but not the patient's name. Is this a problem?
+ Yes; the details could identify the patient and breach confidentiality
- No, because no name was used | Identification is possible from details alone.
- No, because it was posted off duty | Confidentiality applies at all times.
- Only if the patient complains | A breach does not depend on a complaint.
= Any information that could reasonably identify a patient is protected, and sharing it violates privacy law and professional standards.

? A patient speaks only Vietnamese. Her 12-year-old daughter offers to interpret for the discharge teaching. What should the nurse do?
+ Use a qualified medical interpreter
- Accept the daughter's help to save time | Children may mistranslate and should not carry this burden.
- Speak English slowly and loudly | Volume does not overcome a language barrier.
- Give written English instructions only | The patient cannot use them.
= Professional interpreters ensure accuracy and confidentiality and are required for meaningful access to care.

@ 4.8
? What is the most common underlying cause of emergency department crowding?
+ Admitted patients boarding in the ED while waiting for inpatient beds
- Too many low-acuity patients arriving | Low-acuity visits use few resources and contribute little to crowding.
- Slow triage nurses | Triage speed is not the main driver.
- Too few chairs in the waiting room | Seating does not determine flow.
= Crowding is chiefly an output problem: when admitted patients cannot leave the ED, treatment spaces are blocked.

? Which patient is most suitable for a fast-track area?
+ A patient with a simple finger laceration and normal vital signs
- A patient with chest pain and diaphoresis | That patient is high risk and needs a monitored bed.
- A patient with new confusion | That patient needs a full workup.
- A patient with abdominal pain and hypotension | That patient is unstable.
= Fast track is designed for low-acuity patients needing few resources, freeing main ED beds for sicker patients.

? A triage nurse follows an approved protocol to order an ankle radiograph for a patient who meets its criteria before a provider sees him. What is the purpose?
+ To shorten the patient's total length of stay
- To replace the provider's examination | Protocols begin the workup; they do not replace evaluation.
- To allow discharge directly from triage | Disposition still requires a provider.
- To raise the patient's acuity level | Protocols do not change acuity.
= Triage-initiated protocols start diagnostics early so results are ready when the provider evaluates the patient.

? Several treatment rooms are open when a stable patient arrives. Which process is most efficient?
+ Take the patient straight to a room and complete triage and registration there
- Finish a full triage assessment in the triage booth first | That adds a step that creates no value when beds are open.
- Send the patient to the waiting room until called | Waiting is unnecessary with open beds.
- Ask the patient to return when it is less busy | Sending patients away is unsafe.
= Direct bedding removes a bottleneck by moving patients to an open room and completing intake at the bedside.

? Which measure best reflects how well an ED's front-end processes are working?
+ The percentage of patients who leave without being seen
- The number of staff on duty | Staffing is an input, not a flow outcome.
- The number of ambulances arriving | Arrivals describe demand, not performance.
- The size of the department | Size does not measure flow.
= A rising left-without-being-seen rate signals long waits and is a recognized quality and safety indicator.

? An admitted patient with pneumonia has been boarding in the ED for 14 hours. What is the ED nurse's responsibility?
+ Provide ongoing reassessment and carry out the inpatient orders
- Provide only emergency care until a bed is ready | Boarded patients are owed the same standard of care as inpatients.
- Stop monitoring, since the patient is admitted | Admission status does not reduce monitoring needs.
- Hold all scheduled medications until transfer | Missed doses cause harm.
= Boarded patients remain the ED's responsibility and need inpatient-level care, including scheduled medications and reassessment.

? The waiting room is full and the expected wait is three hours. Which nursing practice most improves safety?
+ Reassessing waiting patients at regular intervals and keeping them informed
- Telling patients to wait quietly until called | Passive waiting misses deterioration.
- Posting a sign that staff cannot answer questions | Lack of communication increases risk and frustration.
- Closing triage until rooms open | New arrivals must still be assessed.
= Scheduled reassessment detects deterioration, and communication about delays reduces walkouts and aggression.

? An ambulance crew has waited 40 minutes in the ED hallway with a patient on their stretcher because no bed is free. Who is responsible for the patient?
+ The hospital, from the time the patient arrived on its property
- The ambulance crew, until a bed is assigned | Hospital obligations do not depend on bed assignment.
- No one, until registration is complete | Registration does not create or delay responsibility.
- The patient's primary care provider | That provider has no role in the ED encounter.
= Under EMTALA the hospital's duty begins on arrival; delaying acceptance does not postpone that responsibility.

? Which strategy helps free a monitored bed safely during a surge?
+ Moving a stable, ambulatory patient who is awaiting results to a chair area
- Discharging patients before their results return | That risks missed diagnoses.
- Putting two monitored patients in one bay | That compromises privacy and safety.
- Cancelling tests to speed disposition | That reduces quality of care.
= Vertical or results-pending areas keep stable patients upright and out of beds that sicker patients need.

? An ED is at capacity with eight admitted patients boarding. Which action by hospital leadership would have the greatest effect on ED flow?
+ Moving boarded patients to inpatient units and expediting inpatient discharges
- Adding a second triage nurse | Triage is not the constraint when rooms are blocked.
- Buying more waiting room chairs | Seating does not create treatment capacity.
- Asking ED staff to work faster | Effort cannot overcome a blocked exit.
= Because crowding is driven by output, hospital-wide measures that open inpatient beds relieve the ED most.
