# Domain 9 — Mental Health Emergencies (topics 0-2)

@ 9.0
? A patient with depression says, "Everyone would be better off without me." What is the nurse's best response?
+ "Are you thinking about killing yourself?"
- "You shouldn't talk like that." | Shutting down the statement blocks disclosure.
- "Things will look brighter tomorrow." | False reassurance dismisses the patient's distress.
- "Let's talk about something more cheerful." | Changing the subject avoids a necessary assessment.
= Asking directly about suicide does not plant the idea; it shows concern and allows risk to be assessed.

? Which patient is at highest immediate risk of suicide?
+ A patient who has a loaded gun at home and plans to use it tonight
- A patient who sometimes wishes he could sleep and not wake up | Passive thoughts without a plan carry lower immediate risk.
- A patient who feels sad after a breakup but is making plans for next week | Future orientation is protective.
- A patient who is tearful after a friend's funeral | Grief alone does not indicate high risk.
= A specific plan, access to lethal means and stated intent indicate the greatest imminent danger.

? A patient with active suicidal ideation is placed in an ED room. Which action best protects the patient?
+ Remove belongings and cords and assign continuous one-to-one observation
- Close the door to give privacy | An unobserved patient can self-harm.
- Check on the patient every 30 minutes | Intermittent checks leave long unobserved periods.
- Ask a relative to watch while staff are busy | Family cannot replace trained observation.
= High-risk patients need a ligature-safe environment and constant observation by trained staff.

? Which factor is the strongest single predictor of a future suicide attempt?
+ A previous suicide attempt
- A recent promotion at work | That is not a risk factor.
- A stable marriage | That is protective.
- Regular attendance at religious services | That is often protective.
= A history of attempts is the most powerful predictor, alongside access to means, substance use and recent loss.

? A patient hospitalized for severe depression was withdrawn for weeks. He is now suddenly calm and cheerful and has given his watch to a friend. How should the nurse interpret this?
+ He may have decided to end his life, and the risk is increased
- The depression has resolved | Abrupt calm after deep depression is a warning sign.
- The medication has reached full effect overnight | Antidepressants do not act that suddenly.
- No further assessment is needed | Reassessment is urgent.
= Sudden peace and giving away possessions can follow a decision to die; rising energy may also enable a plan.

? An ED introduces a brief validated suicide screening tool for all patients. What is the main reason for universal screening?
+ Many people at risk present with unrelated physical complaints
- It replaces psychiatric evaluation | Screening identifies who needs fuller assessment.
- It is required only for patients with known depression | Risk is not limited to known psychiatric patients.
- It shortens the triage process | Its purpose is detection, not speed.
= Many people who die by suicide visit health care shortly beforehand for other reasons, and screening identifies otherwise hidden risk.

? A patient tells the nurse, "When I get out of here I'm going to shoot my ex-wife. I know where she works." What must the nurse do?
+ Notify the provider at once so that the threatened person and authorities can be warned
- Keep the statement confidential | A credible threat to an identifiable person overrides confidentiality.
- Tell the patient that such thoughts are normal | That minimizes a serious threat.
- Document it and take no other action | Documentation alone does not protect the intended victim.
= A specific threat against an identifiable person triggers a duty to protect that person.

? A patient who came in voluntarily with a detailed suicide plan now says he feels fine and wants to go home. What should the nurse do?
+ Keep him under observation and notify the provider to evaluate for an involuntary hold
- Let him sign out against medical advice | He is at imminent risk of harm.
- Call a taxi for him | Discharge is unsafe.
- Tell him he is under arrest | A hold is a clinical process, not an arrest.
= A patient at imminent risk may not simply leave; evaluation for emergency detention protects him.

? A patient is being discharged after a suicidal crisis. Which intervention is most effective in reducing future risk?
+ A written safety plan with removal of firearms and stockpiled medication from the home
- A signed promise not to attempt suicide | No-suicide contracts have not been shown to work.
- A leaflet about healthy eating | It does not address suicide risk.
- Advice to keep busy | General advice does not reduce risk.
= Safety planning with lethal-means restriction and crisis contacts, including the 988 line, reduces subsequent attempts.

? A 19-year-old took "a handful of pills" two hours ago and now says it was "nothing." She is alert with normal vital signs. Which test must be sent even though she has no symptoms?
+ An acetaminophen level
- A thyroid panel | It does not identify a dangerous ingestion.
- A lipid panel | It is irrelevant.
- A vitamin D level | It is irrelevant.
= Acetaminophen is commonly co-ingested, causes no early symptoms and is fatal if untreated, so a level is checked in every intentional overdose.

? A 15-year-old tells the nurse she plans to take her mother's pills and adds, "Please don't tell my parents." What is the best response?
+ "I can't keep this secret because I need to keep you safe."
- "I promise I won't tell anyone." | The nurse cannot keep confidential a risk of serious harm.
- "You're just looking for attention." | That is dismissive and damages trust.
- "Let's wait and see how you feel next week." | Delay leaves her at risk.
= Confidentiality does not extend to imminent risk of harm; honesty about that limit preserves trust.

@ 9.1
? A patient is pacing, shouting and refusing to return to his room. What is the first-line intervention?
+ Verbal de-escalation
- Physical restraint | Restraint is a last resort after less restrictive measures fail.
- Intramuscular sedation without discussion | Medication follows attempts to de-escalate and offers of oral medication.
- Calling the police to remove him | He is a patient who needs care.
= Verbal de-escalation is the first step and often avoids the need for restraint or forced medication.

? Which approach is most effective during verbal de-escalation?
+ One staff member speaks calmly in short sentences from a respectful distance
- Several staff give instructions at the same time | Multiple voices confuse and escalate.
- The nurse stands very close to show concern | Crowding feels threatening.
- The nurse uses detailed medical explanations | Long explanations overwhelm an agitated person.
= A single calm voice, simple language, personal space and listening help an agitated patient regain control.

? A 62-year-old with no psychiatric history is suddenly agitated and combative. Before assuming a psychiatric cause, which assessments are essential?
+ Blood glucose, oxygen saturation and a set of vital signs
- A personality questionnaire | It does not identify a medical cause.
- A family history of mental illness only | History does not exclude acute medical problems.
- A handwriting sample | It is irrelevant.
= Hypoglycemia, hypoxia, infection, head injury and intoxication commonly present as agitation and must be excluded first.

? An agitated but cooperative patient agrees that he needs "something to calm down." What should the nurse offer first?
+ An oral medication
- An intramuscular injection held down by staff | Forced injection is unnecessary when he will take medication.
- Four-point restraints | Restraint is not indicated for a cooperative patient.
- Nothing until he is fully calm | Timely medication prevents escalation.
= Offering oral medication respects the patient's autonomy, preserves trust and works nearly as fast.

? A severely agitated patient remains a danger to staff despite de-escalation and refuses oral medication. Which medications are commonly used?
+ An intramuscular antipsychotic, with or without a benzodiazepine
- An oral antidepressant | Antidepressants do not act acutely.
- A stimulant | Stimulants worsen agitation.
- An oral lithium tablet | Lithium has no acute calming effect.
= Intramuscular agents such as haloperidol, olanzapine or droperidol, sometimes with a benzodiazepine, are used for severe agitation.

? Before giving IV haloperidol to an agitated patient, which test result is most important to review?
+ The QT interval on the ECG
- The serum amylase | It is not affected by haloperidol.
- The hemoglobin A1c | It is irrelevant here.
- The prothrombin time | It is not affected.
= Haloperidol and droperidol prolong the QT interval and can cause torsades de pointes.

? An 88-year-old with dementia becomes agitated in the evening. Which approach should be tried first?
+ A quiet room, familiar objects, reorientation and attention to pain or toileting needs
- A large dose of lorazepam | Benzodiazepines often cause paradoxical agitation and falls in older adults.
- Wrist restraints | Restraints increase agitation and injury.
- Leaving her alone in a bright, busy hallway | Stimulation worsens agitation.
= Non-drug measures and treatment of unmet needs are first-line for agitation in dementia.

? An agitated patient is moved from a noisy hallway bed to a quiet room with dimmed lights. What is the purpose?
+ Reducing stimulation helps lower agitation
- It punishes the behaviour | Seclusion as punishment is unethical.
- It hides the patient from other staff | The patient must remain observable.
- It allows monitoring to stop | Monitoring continues.
= Noise, crowds and bright light intensify agitation; a calm environment supports de-escalation.

? A man is brought in by police after a struggle. He is delirious, extremely agitated, sweating heavily, with a temperature of 40.4 C and HR 158. What is the priority?
+ Rapid sedation, cooling and continuous monitoring
- Holding him face down until he tires | Prone restraint with ongoing struggle risks sudden death.
- A psychiatric interview before any treatment | He has a life-threatening medical emergency.
- Waiting in a seclusion room for the agitation to pass | Untreated hyperthermia and acidosis can be fatal.
= Severe agitated delirium with hyperthermia carries a risk of sudden cardiac arrest; sedation, cooling and resuscitation are urgent.

? An hour after an antipsychotic injection, a patient says he "can't sit still," shifts constantly from foot to foot and feels an inner restlessness. What is the most likely cause?
+ Akathisia from the medication
- Worsening psychosis | The restlessness is a drug effect.
- Opioid withdrawal | The timing follows the injection.
- Normal behaviour | The new restlessness is a side effect.
= Akathisia is an extrapyramidal effect easily mistaken for agitation; more antipsychotic makes it worse.

? A patient has been sedated with intramuscular medication for severe agitation. What monitoring is essential afterwards?
+ Airway, respiratory rate, oxygen saturation and level of sedation
- A weight measurement | It does not detect over-sedation.
- Bowel sounds every hour | They are not the priority.
- None, once the patient is asleep | Over-sedation can cause respiratory depression.
= Sedated patients need close observation for airway compromise and respiratory depression.

@ 9.2
? A patient with schizophrenia says, "The voices are telling me to do something." What should the nurse ask next?
+ "What are the voices telling you to do?"
- "Why do you believe the voices?" | "Why" questions are confrontational and unhelpful.
- "Can you ignore them?" | That does not assess danger.
- "Are the voices male or female?" | That detail does not establish risk.
= Command hallucinations may instruct the patient to harm himself or others, so their content is assessed first.

? A patient insists that the hospital has implanted a tracking chip in his arm. Which response is most therapeutic?
+ "That must be frightening. I don't see a chip, and you are safe here."
- "That's impossible, and you know it." | Arguing strengthens the belief and damages trust.
- "Yes, the chip was placed yesterday." | Agreeing reinforces the delusion.
- "Let's X-ray your arm to prove you're wrong." | Trying to disprove a delusion is ineffective.
= The nurse acknowledges the feeling, states reality briefly and does not argue or agree.

? A 21-year-old with no psychiatric history is brought in hearing voices and behaving bizarrely. What is needed before a psychiatric diagnosis is made?
+ A medical evaluation including a drug screen
- Immediate transfer to a psychiatric unit | Medical causes must be excluded first.
- A long-acting antipsychotic injection | The cause has not been established.
- Discharge with a counselling referral | He needs evaluation now.
= First-episode psychosis can be caused by drugs, infection, metabolic disease or neurologic conditions.

? A patient started haloperidol four days ago. He now has a temperature of 40.1 C, rigid muscles, confusion and a labile blood pressure. What should the nurse suspect?
+ Neuroleptic malignant syndrome
- Acute dystonia | Dystonia causes muscle spasm without fever or autonomic instability.
- Serotonin syndrome | That follows serotonergic drugs and features clonus rather than lead-pipe rigidity.
- A viral illness | The combination after an antipsychotic is characteristic.
= Fever, severe rigidity, altered mental status and autonomic instability after an antipsychotic is a life-threatening emergency; the drug is stopped.

? Two hours after an intramuscular antipsychotic, a young man's neck twists painfully to one side and his eyes roll upward. Which medication should the nurse anticipate?
+ Diphenhydramine or benztropine
- More of the antipsychotic | The drug caused the reaction.
- Naloxone | Opioids are not involved.
- Epinephrine | This is not an allergic reaction.
= Acute dystonia responds within minutes to an anticholinergic agent.

? A patient taking clozapine reports a sore throat and a temperature of 38.6 C. Which test is most important?
+ A complete blood count with differential
- A serum lithium level | He is not taking lithium.
- A urine drug screen | It does not address the concern.
- A thyroid panel | It is not the priority.
= Clozapine can cause severe neutropenia; fever or sore throat requires an urgent neutrophil count.

? A patient who has taken antipsychotics for 15 years has involuntary lip smacking, tongue protrusion and grimacing. What is this condition?
+ Tardive dyskinesia
- Acute dystonia | That occurs within days of starting treatment.
- Akathisia | That is an inner restlessness with pacing.
- Parkinson's disease | The pattern and history point to a drug effect.
= Tardive dyskinesia is a late, often irreversible movement disorder from long-term dopamine blockade.

? A patient is brushing at his skin, saying insects are crawling under it, and sees animals on the walls. How should the nurse interpret these hallucinations?
+ Tactile and visual hallucinations suggest a medical or substance-related cause
- They are typical of schizophrenia | Schizophrenia most often causes auditory hallucinations.
- They indicate malingering | The symptoms have a physical basis.
- They need no workup | Withdrawal and intoxication must be excluded.
= Visual and tactile hallucinations point to delirium, withdrawal or stimulant toxicity rather than a primary psychotic disorder.

? A patient with schizophrenia who was stable for two years is brought in acutely psychotic. Which question is most important?
+ "Have you been taking your medication?"
- "Have you changed your diet?" | Diet is not a usual cause of relapse.
- "Have you been exercising?" | Exercise is not a cause of relapse.
- "Have you moved recently?" | It may be a stressor but is not the leading cause.
= Stopping antipsychotic medication is the most common reason for relapse.

? Which approach is best when caring for a suspicious, paranoid patient?
+ Explain each action before doing it and keep a comfortable distance
- Whisper with colleagues just outside the room | Whispering feeds suspicion.
- Touch the patient's arm to reassure him | Unexpected touch may be perceived as an attack.
- Tell small untruths to keep him calm | Dishonesty destroys trust.
= Honesty, consistency, clear explanation and respect for personal space reduce fear.

? Ten days after giving birth, a woman is confused, has not slept and believes her baby is possessed. What should the nurse recognize?
+ This is a psychiatric emergency with risk to mother and infant
- This is the common "baby blues" | Baby blues are mild and resolve within two weeks without psychosis.
- This will pass with a good night's sleep | Postpartum psychosis needs urgent treatment.
- This is normal adjustment to parenthood | Delusions about the infant are never normal.
= Postpartum psychosis carries a risk of suicide and infanticide; the mother is not left alone with the baby and is admitted.
