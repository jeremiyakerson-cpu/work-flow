# Domain 7 — Gastrointestinal Emergencies (topics 6-7)

@ 7.6
? A 2-year-old with two days of vomiting and diarrhea has sunken eyes, dry lips, HR 150 and has passed urine once in 12 hours. She is alert but irritable, with a capillary refill of 2 to 3 seconds. How should her dehydration be classified?
+ Moderate
- None | Several signs of dehydration are present.
- Mild | Mild dehydration shows few or no signs beyond thirst.
- Severe with shock | Shock would show lethargy, mottling and markedly delayed refill.
= Tachycardia, sunken eyes, dry mucosa and reduced urine output without shock indicate moderate dehydration.

? A child with mild to moderate dehydration from gastroenteritis is tolerating small sips after ondansetron. Which rehydration method is preferred?
+ Oral rehydration solution in small, frequent amounts
- IV fluids for every dehydrated child | Oral rehydration is as effective and less invasive when tolerated.
- Undiluted apple juice and soda | High-sugar drinks worsen osmotic diarrhea.
- Plain water only | Water lacks the sodium and glucose needed for absorption.
= Oral rehydration solution given by spoon or syringe every few minutes is first-line for mild to moderate dehydration.

? A lethargic 12 kg toddler has mottled skin, a capillary refill of 5 seconds and no urine output for 14 hours. Which initial fluid order should the nurse anticipate?
+ 240 mL of isotonic crystalloid as a rapid bolus
- 60 mL of dextrose 5% in water | The volume is too small and the fluid is hypotonic.
- 1,000 mL of normal saline | That adult volume would overload the child.
- Oral rehydration solution by cup | A child in shock cannot be rehydrated orally.
= Hypovolemic shock in a child is treated with 20 mL/kg of isotonic crystalloid, here 240 mL, then reassessment.

? An 78-year-old finished a course of clindamycin a week ago and now has profuse watery, foul-smelling diarrhea with cramps and fever. Which precautions should the nurse start?
+ Contact precautions with soap-and-water hand washing
- Droplet precautions | The organism is not spread by droplets.
- Airborne precautions | It is not airborne.
- Standard precautions with alcohol hand rub only | Alcohol does not kill the spores.
= Clostridioides difficile forms spores that survive alcohol rub; gown, gloves and hand washing are required.

? A patient with fever and bloody diarrhea asks for loperamide. What should the nurse explain?
+ Antimotility drugs can worsen invasive infections and are avoided
- Loperamide is recommended for all diarrhea | It is unsafe with fever and blood.
- A double dose will work faster | A larger dose increases the danger.
- It should be taken with antibiotics | The combination does not make it safe here.
= Slowing the bowel prolongs contact with toxins and invasive organisms and can precipitate toxic megacolon.

? A 4-year-old had bloody diarrhea after eating undercooked hamburger. A week later he is pale and puffy with tiny bruises and very little urine. Which complication should the nurse suspect?
+ Hemolytic uremic syndrome
- Appendicitis | That would cause localized abdominal pain and fever.
- Lactose intolerance | That causes bloating and loose stools only.
- Iron-deficiency anemia | That does not cause bruising or oliguria acutely.
= Shiga toxin-producing E. coli can cause hemolytic anemia, low platelets and acute kidney injury.

? Twelve residents of a care home develop vomiting and watery diarrhea within 24 hours. Which measure best limits the spread?
+ Contact precautions with hand washing and bleach-based cleaning
- Airborne isolation for all residents | The virus spreads by contact and contaminated surfaces.
- Antibiotics for every resident | The illness is viral.
- Alcohol hand rub alone | Alcohol is less effective against norovirus.
= Norovirus spreads easily through contact and surfaces; soap-and-water washing and chlorine disinfectants are needed.

? An 86-year-old has had diarrhea for four days. Which finding best indicates significant dehydration?
+ Dizziness on standing with a rise in heart rate and new confusion
- Loose skin on the back of the hand | Skin turgor is unreliable in older adults.
- A complaint of feeling tired | Fatigue is non-specific.
- A dry mouth while breathing through the mouth | Mouth breathing dries the mucosa regardless of hydration.
= Orthostatic changes and altered mentation are more reliable than skin turgor in older adults.

? A 24-year-old who uses cannabis daily has repeated bouts of severe vomiting that ease only while he stands in a hot shower. Antiemetics have not helped. What is the most effective long-term treatment?
+ Stopping cannabis use
- Increasing cannabis to settle the nausea | Continued use perpetuates the syndrome.
- Daily ondansetron for life | Standard antiemetics are often ineffective.
- A gluten-free diet | Diet is not the cause.
= Cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome causes cyclic vomiting relieved by hot bathing; it resolves only with cessation.

? A patient with severe diarrhea has a potassium of 2.7 mEq/L, muscle weakness and U waves on the ECG. IV potassium chloride is ordered. Which administration practice is correct?
+ Give it diluted by infusion pump at a controlled rate with cardiac monitoring
- Give it undiluted by IV push | A potassium bolus can cause fatal dysrhythmia.
- Give it intramuscularly | Potassium is never given by injection into muscle.
- Add it to a bag that is already running without mixing | Poor mixing delivers a concentrated bolus.
= IV potassium is always diluted and infused slowly, usually no faster than 10 mEq per hour through a peripheral line.

? Which assessment finding in a 3-month-old with diarrhea is most concerning for dehydration?
+ A sunken fontanelle with no wet diaper for eight hours
- Crying with tears | Tears indicate reasonable hydration.
- A moist mouth | Moist mucosa is reassuring.
- A soft, flat fontanelle | That is normal.
= A depressed fontanelle and absent urine output indicate significant fluid loss in an infant.

? Six people develop sudden vomiting and cramps three hours after a picnic where potato salad sat in the sun. They have no fever. What is the most likely cause?
+ A preformed bacterial toxin in the food
- A viral illness with a two-day incubation | The onset is too rapid.
- Appendicitis | That would not affect six people together.
- A parasitic infection | Parasites have a much longer incubation.
= Staphylococcal toxin causes vomiting within one to six hours of eating contaminated food and resolves within a day.

@ 7.7
? A patient has a groin bulge that appears on coughing and slides back in when he lies down. How is this hernia described?
+ Reducible
- Incarcerated | An incarcerated hernia cannot be pushed back.
- Strangulated | A strangulated hernia has lost its blood supply.
- Perforated | That term does not describe a hernia type.
= A reducible hernia returns to the abdominal cavity spontaneously or with gentle pressure.

? A patient has a firm, very tender groin mass with dusky red skin over it. He has a fever and is vomiting. What should the nurse anticipate?
+ Emergency surgery
- Forceful manual reduction | Pushing dead bowel back into the abdomen causes peritonitis.
- Discharge with a truss | A support does not treat ischemic bowel.
- A warm compress and reassessment tomorrow | Delay leads to bowel necrosis.
= Skin changes, severe tenderness and systemic signs indicate strangulation; reduction is not attempted and surgery is urgent.

? A patient has a tender groin hernia that will not reduce. There is no skin discoloration, fever or vomiting. Which measures help a provider attempt reduction?
+ Analgesia, a head-down position and a cold pack
- Standing the patient up and asking him to cough | Standing and coughing push the hernia out.
- A heating pad and a large meal | Heat increases swelling and food is unsafe before possible surgery.
- Vigorous massage of the lump | Force risks bowel injury.
= Pain relief, gravity and reduced swelling relax the area and make gentle reduction more likely to succeed.

? Which hernia type is the most common overall?
+ Indirect inguinal
- Femoral | Femoral hernias are less common.
- Umbilical in adults | They are less common than inguinal hernias.
- Obturator | Obturator hernias are rare.
= Indirect inguinal hernias, passing through the internal ring, are the most frequent in both sexes and especially in men.

? A 72-year-old woman has a small, painful lump just below the inguinal ligament. Why is this hernia treated with particular urgency?
+ Femoral hernias have a narrow neck and strangulate readily
- Femoral hernias always contain the bladder | That is not the reason.
- Femoral hernias occur only in children | They occur mainly in older women.
- Femoral hernias cannot be repaired | They are repaired surgically.
= The rigid, narrow femoral canal makes incarceration and strangulation common.

? The parents of a 6-month-old ask about the soft bulge at his navel that enlarges when he cries and is easily pushed in. What should the nurse tell them?
+ Most close on their own by about age four or five
- Tape a coin over it to speed closure | Taping does not help and irritates the skin.
- He needs emergency surgery today | A reducible umbilical hernia is not an emergency.
- It will get larger for life | Most resolve spontaneously.
= Umbilical hernias in infants usually close without treatment; a hard, tender or irreducible bulge needs prompt review.

? A patient who had a laparotomy a year ago has a bulge along the scar that enlarges on straining. What type of hernia is this?
+ Incisional
- Inguinal | That occurs in the groin.
- Hiatal | That occurs at the diaphragm.
- Femoral | That occurs below the inguinal ligament.
= An incisional hernia develops through a weakened surgical wound, with obesity, infection and heavy strain as risk factors.

? A patient with a known hiatal hernia has heartburn that is worse at night. Which teaching is most helpful?
+ Raise the head of the bed and avoid eating within three hours of lying down
- Lie flat straight after meals | Lying flat promotes reflux.
- Eat one large meal late in the evening | Large late meals worsen symptoms.
- Wear a tight belt for support | Tight clothing raises abdominal pressure.
= Elevation and smaller, earlier meals reduce reflux through the lax gastroesophageal junction.

? An 80-year-old woman has vomiting, distension and colicky pain with no history of abdominal surgery. Which part of the examination is essential?
+ Inspecting and palpating both groins
- Testing visual acuity | It is unrelated.
- Checking ankle reflexes | They do not explain obstruction.
- Measuring head circumference | It is irrelevant in an adult.
= A small incarcerated groin hernia, often femoral, is easily missed and is a common cause of obstruction in a patient with no previous surgery.

? A 2-month-old boy is inconsolable and has a firm swelling in the right groin that does not reduce when he is calm. What should the nurse do?
+ Arrange urgent surgical assessment
- Reassure the parents that it will close by age five | That applies to umbilical, not inguinal, hernias.
- Tell the parents to return if it is still there next month | Incarceration in infants threatens the bowel and testis.
- Apply a tight nappy for support | Compression does not treat incarceration.
= Inguinal hernias in infants incarcerate often and never close spontaneously; an irreducible one is an emergency.

? A patient with a reducible inguinal hernia is discharged to await elective repair. Which symptom should prompt an immediate return?
+ A lump that becomes painful and cannot be pushed back
- A lump that is smaller in the morning | That is expected.
- Mild aching after a long day standing | That is expected.
- A lump that disappears when lying down | That is the definition of reducible.
= Irreducibility with pain, vomiting or skin colour change suggests incarceration or strangulation.

? An incarcerated hernia was reduced in the ED an hour ago. Why is the patient kept for observation?
+ Bowel that was already compromised may have been returned to the abdomen
- The hernia always comes back within the hour | Recurrence is possible but is not the main concern.
- Sedation takes a full day to wear off | That is not accurate.
- Reduction causes urinary retention | That is not the usual concern.
= Increasing pain, fever or peritoneal signs after reduction suggest ischemic bowel and need surgical review.
