# Domain 11 — Head, Eye, Ear, Nose & Throat Emergencies (topics 0-2)

@ 11.0
? A man arrives with a brisk nosebleed and is holding a tissue loosely under his nose. What should the triage nurse instruct him to do?
+ Sit upright, lean forward and pinch the soft part of the nose firmly for at least 10 minutes
- Tilt the head back and pinch the bridge | Blood runs down the throat, and the bony bridge cannot be compressed.
- Lie flat with a cold pack on the forehead | Lying flat increases swallowing of blood and aspiration risk.
- Blow the nose hard every minute to clear clots | Repeated blowing dislodges the clot.
= Continuous pressure on the soft nasal alae compresses the anterior septal vessels where most nosebleeds arise.

? Where do most nosebleeds originate?
+ The anterior nasal septum
- The posterior nasal cavity | Posterior bleeds are less common and more severe.
- The maxillary sinus | The sinuses are not the usual source.
- The nasopharynx | That is not the usual source.
= About nine in ten nosebleeds come from Kiesselbach's plexus on the anterior septum.

? An 82-year-old on warfarin has bleeding from both nostrils that continues despite correct pressure, with blood running down the back of the throat. What should the nurse suspect?
+ A posterior nosebleed
- A simple anterior bleed | Anterior bleeds usually stop with pressure.
- A sinus infection | Infection does not cause brisk bilateral bleeding.
- Bleeding gums | The source is nasal.
= Posterior bleeding is heavier, drains into the pharynx and is more common in older, hypertensive or anticoagulated patients.

? A patient has posterior nasal packing placed. What is the appropriate disposition and monitoring?
+ Admission with continuous oxygen saturation and cardiac monitoring
- Discharge with advice to return in a week | Posterior packs can cause hypoxia and bradycardia.
- Observation in the waiting room | Close monitoring is required.
- Discharge with oral antihistamines | They do not address the risks.
= Posterior packing can compromise the airway and trigger a vagal response, so patients are admitted and monitored.

? Pressure alone has not stopped an anterior nosebleed. Which topical medication is commonly applied next?
+ A vasoconstrictor spray such as oxymetazoline
- A steroid nasal spray | It has no immediate hemostatic effect.
- A saline rinse | Rinsing dislodges clot.
- An antihistamine spray | It does not constrict vessels.
= A topical vasoconstrictor followed by further pressure stops most anterior bleeds; cautery or packing follows if needed.

? A patient is discharged with anterior nasal packing in place. Which instruction is correct?
+ Do not blow the nose, and sneeze with the mouth open
- Remove the packing at home tonight | The pack is removed by a clinician after a few days.
- Take ibuprofen for discomfort | NSAIDs impair clotting.
- Lift weights as usual | Straining can restart bleeding.
= Avoiding pressure changes and antiplatelet drugs protects the clot until the pack is removed.

? A patient on warfarin has a nosebleed. Which laboratory test is most relevant?
+ INR
- Amylase | It is unrelated.
- Thyroid-stimulating hormone | It is unrelated.
- Lipid panel | It is unrelated.
= An elevated INR explains difficult bleeding and guides reversal or dose adjustment.

? A 6-year-old has frequent nosebleeds along with easy bruising and bleeding gums. What should the nurse consider?
+ An underlying bleeding disorder
- Nose picking alone | That does not explain the bruising and gum bleeding.
- Seasonal allergies | They do not cause bruising.
- A normal childhood finding | The combination warrants evaluation.
= Recurrent nosebleeds with other bleeding signs suggest a clotting or platelet disorder.

? A patient with a nosebleed has soaked several towels and now has HR 128 and BP 88/50. What is the priority?
+ Establish IV access and send blood for type and screen while bleeding control continues
- Wait until the bleeding stops before starting an IV | Shock needs immediate treatment.
- Give an oral iron tablet | Iron does not treat acute blood loss.
- Discharge once the bleeding slows | The patient is in hemorrhagic shock.
= Significant blood loss from epistaxis is resuscitated like any other hemorrhage.

? Two days after nasal packing was placed, a patient has a high fever, a diffuse red rash, vomiting and BP 84/48. What should the nurse suspect?
+ Toxic shock syndrome
- An expected reaction to the packing | These are signs of a life-threatening illness.
- A common cold | A cold does not cause shock and rash.
- A food allergy | The timing and findings point to the packing.
= Nasal packing can harbour toxin-producing staphylococci; the pack is removed and the patient treated for shock.

@ 11.1
? A 12-year-old's permanent front tooth was knocked out 15 minutes ago. The tooth is in the parent's hand. What is the best immediate action?
+ Rinse it gently, hold it by the crown and replace it in the socket
- Scrub the root clean with a brush | Scrubbing destroys the ligament cells needed for reattachment.
- Wrap it in dry gauze | Drying kills the root surface cells within minutes.
- Soak it in tap water | Water damages the root cells.
= Reimplantation within about 30-60 minutes gives the best outcome; if not possible, the tooth is stored in milk or a tooth-preservation solution.

? A 3-year-old's primary front tooth is knocked out. What should the nurse advise?
+ The tooth is not reimplanted
- Push it back into the socket at once | Reimplanting a primary tooth can damage the developing permanent tooth.
- Store it in milk for reimplantation tomorrow | Primary teeth are not replaced.
- Glue it in with tissue adhesive | That is inappropriate.
= Avulsed primary teeth are left out to protect the permanent tooth bud beneath.

? A patient has a fractured tooth with a pink, bleeding spot visible in the centre and severe pain with air or cold. Why is this a dental emergency?
+ The pulp is exposed and will become infected
- Only the enamel is chipped | An enamel-only fracture is painless and not urgent.
- The tooth will grow back | Permanent teeth do not regenerate.
- The pain will pass without treatment | Exposed pulp needs covering and urgent dental care.
= A fracture into the pulp exposes nerve and vessels; it is covered and referred for immediate dental treatment.

? A patient with a dental abscess has swelling under the jaw, cannot open the mouth more than a finger's width and has difficulty swallowing. What does this indicate?
+ Spread of infection into the deep spaces of the neck
- An expected course for a toothache | Trismus and dysphagia are warning signs.
- A reaction to analgesics | The findings indicate spreading infection.
- Temporomandibular joint strain | It does not cause fever and swelling under the jaw.
= Trismus, dysphagia and submandibular swelling signal deep neck infection that threatens the airway.

? Three days after a wisdom tooth extraction, a patient has severe throbbing pain radiating to the ear and a foul taste. The socket looks empty. What is this condition?
+ Dry socket
- A normal healing socket | Normal healing does not cause escalating pain on day three.
- A dental abscess | An abscess causes swelling and pus.
- A fractured jaw | That would follow trauma with malocclusion.
= Loss of the clot exposes bone; the socket is irrigated and packed with a medicated dressing.

? A patient is bleeding steadily from a tooth socket two hours after an extraction. What should the nurse do first?
+ Have the patient bite firmly on folded gauze for 20 to 30 minutes
- Ask the patient to rinse and spit repeatedly | Rinsing and spitting dislodge the clot.
- Give a drink through a straw | Suction pulls the clot out.
- Apply heat to the cheek | Heat increases bleeding.
= Sustained direct pressure over the socket controls most post-extraction bleeding.

? A 22-year-old under heavy stress has painful, bleeding gums with a grey film, punched-out ulcers between the teeth and very bad breath. What is the most likely condition?
+ Acute necrotizing ulcerative gingivitis
- Simple plaque gingivitis | That is painless and lacks ulceration.
- Oral thrush | Thrush forms white plaques that wipe off.
- Herpes labialis | That affects the lips with vesicles.
= "Trench mouth" is a bacterial infection of the gums treated with debridement, antiseptic rinses and antibiotics.

? A 67-year-old man with diabetes reports aching in his lower jaw that comes on when he climbs stairs and eases with rest. His teeth look healthy. What should the nurse do?
+ Obtain a 12-lead ECG
- Refer him to a dentist next month | Exertional jaw pain can be angina.
- Offer a warm salt-water rinse | It does not address a cardiac cause.
- Reassure him that it is arthritis | A dangerous cause has not been excluded.
= Jaw pain brought on by exertion and relieved by rest is an anginal equivalent until proven otherwise.

? After a fall a child has a broken front tooth, and a piece is missing. He has a new cough. What should the nurse anticipate?
+ A chest radiograph to look for an aspirated fragment
- Reassurance that the piece was swallowed | A cough raises concern for aspiration.
- A search of the playground only | The fragment may be in the airway.
- A dental referral in two weeks without imaging | Aspiration must be excluded first.
= Missing tooth fragments may be embedded in the lip or aspirated; a cough makes aspiration likely.

? A patient has severe toothache with no swelling or fever. Which analgesic approach is first-line?
+ An NSAID, alone or with acetaminophen
- An opioid as the first choice | NSAIDs are at least as effective for dental pain and safer.
- Aspirin held against the gum | Aspirin burns the mucosa.
- An antibiotic alone for pain | Antibiotics do not relieve pain without infection.
= Dental pain is inflammatory, and NSAIDs with acetaminophen outperform opioids for most patients.

@ 11.2
? A worker arrives with drain cleaner splashed in both eyes. What should the nurse do first?
+ Begin copious irrigation immediately
- Measure visual acuity | Acuity is deferred in a chemical injury.
- Take a full history of the product | History is obtained while irrigation is under way.
- Apply an eye patch | A patch holds the chemical against the eye.
= A chemical splash is the one eye emergency in which treatment precedes visual acuity; every second of delay increases damage.

? How does the nurse know when irrigation of a chemical eye injury can stop?
+ The tear pH is between 7.0 and 7.4 several minutes after irrigation is paused
- One litre of fluid has been used | Volume alone is not an endpoint.
- The patient says the pain has gone | Pain relief does not show that the chemical is cleared.
- Ten minutes have passed | Alkali burns may need far longer.
= Irrigation continues until the pH is neutral and stays neutral on rechecking.

? Which eye injury carries the worst prognosis?
+ A splash of concentrated alkali
- A splash of dilute vinegar | A weak acid causes a superficial injury.
- A splash of soapy water | It is a mild irritant.
- A speck of dust | It causes minor irritation.
= Alkalis penetrate the cornea rapidly and continue to destroy deeper structures.

? A carpenter was struck in the eye by a nail. The pupil is teardrop shaped and dark tissue is visible at the edge of the cornea. What should the nurse do?
+ Place a rigid shield over the eye without pressure and keep him NPO
- Apply a pressure patch | Pressure can push out the contents of the eye.
- Instil antibiotic drops | Nothing is put into an open globe.
- Measure the eye pressure | Tonometry is contraindicated.
= A teardrop pupil and exposed uveal tissue indicate an open globe; the eye is shielded and vomiting prevented.

? A patient hit by a racquetball has a layer of blood settling in the lower part of the anterior chamber. How should the nurse position the patient?
+ Head of the bed raised 30 to 45 degrees
- Flat on the back | Lying flat spreads blood across the visual axis.
- Prone | It increases pressure in the eye.
- Trendelenburg | It raises pressure in the eye.
= With a hyphema, elevation lets blood settle inferiorly; the eye is shielded and aspirin and NSAIDs avoided.

? A patient has eye pain, tearing and a gritty sensation after being poked by a child's fingernail. Which test confirms a corneal abrasion?
+ Fluorescein staining under blue light
- A pupil dilation test | It does not show an abrasion.
- A colour vision test | It does not show an abrasion.
- A visual field test | It does not show an abrasion.
= Fluorescein pools in areas where the corneal epithelium is missing and glows green under cobalt-blue light.

? A patient with a corneal abrasion asks to take the numbing eye drops home because they relieved the pain. What should the nurse explain?
+ Repeated use delays healing and can damage the cornea
- They are safe to use hourly for a week | Prolonged use is toxic to the cornea.
- They are available over the counter | They are not dispensed for home use.
- They are stronger than oral analgesics and preferred | They are not prescribed for home use.
= Topical anaesthetics impair epithelial healing and remove the protective blink reflex.

? A contact lens wearer has a painful red eye with a white spot on the cornea. What should the nurse anticipate?
+ Same-day ophthalmology review and antibiotic drops that cover Pseudomonas
- Reinserting the lens as a bandage | Lens wear must stop.
- An eye patch and next-week follow-up | Patching promotes bacterial growth, and delay risks perforation.
- Lubricating drops only | A corneal ulcer needs antibiotics.
= Contact lens-related keratitis is often caused by Pseudomonas and can destroy the cornea within a day.

? A man felt something strike his eye while hammering metal. His vision is slightly blurred and only a tiny wound is seen. Which imaging study is appropriate?
+ A CT scan of the orbits
- An MRI of the orbits | A metal fragment can move in the magnetic field and cause blindness.
- No imaging, as the wound is small | High-velocity fragments penetrate with minimal external signs.
- A dental radiograph | It does not image the globe.
= Metal-on-metal injuries can drive a fragment into the eye; CT locates it and MRI is avoided.

? After blunt trauma, a patient has a bulging, rock-hard eye with rapidly falling vision and severe pain. Which procedure should the nurse prepare for?
+ An emergency lateral canthotomy
- A routine eye patch | It does not relieve the pressure.
- A slit-lamp examination tomorrow | Vision will be lost within about two hours.
- Irrigation with saline | There is no chemical injury.
= Bleeding behind the eye compresses the optic nerve; releasing the lateral canthal tendon decompresses the orbit.
