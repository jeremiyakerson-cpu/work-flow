# Domain 8 — Genitourinary, Gynecological & Obstetrical Emergencies (topics 6-8)

@ 8.6
? A woman at 33 weeks has BP 168/112, a severe headache, blurred vision and pain under the right ribs. What do these findings indicate?
+ Preeclampsia with severe features
- Normal third-trimester discomforts | These symptoms signal end-organ involvement.
- Migraine | Migraine does not explain severe hypertension and right upper quadrant pain.
- Gallstones only | Gallstones do not cause hypertension and visual change.
= Blood pressure of 160/110 or more with headache, visual disturbance or right upper quadrant pain after 20 weeks defines severe preeclampsia.

? A pregnant woman at 36 weeks has a generalized tonic-clonic seizure in triage. Which medication is first-line?
+ IV magnesium sulfate
- IV phenytoin | It is less effective than magnesium for eclampsia.
- IV lorazepam as the only treatment | Benzodiazepines are second-line if seizures persist.
- Oral levetiracetam | Oral therapy cannot be given during a seizure.
= Magnesium sulfate, a loading dose followed by an infusion, is the treatment of choice for eclamptic seizures.

? A patient on a magnesium sulfate infusion has absent knee reflexes and a respiratory rate of 9. What should the nurse do first?
+ Stop the infusion
- Increase the infusion rate | More magnesium deepens the toxicity.
- Recheck in an hour | Respiratory arrest may follow.
- Give a diuretic | Diuresis is not the immediate treatment.
= Loss of deep tendon reflexes and respiratory depression are signs of magnesium toxicity; the infusion is stopped and calcium gluconate prepared.

? Which assessments should be made at least hourly during a magnesium sulfate infusion?
+ Deep tendon reflexes, respiratory rate and urine output
- Pupil size, bowel sounds and skin turgor over the forearm | These do not detect toxicity.
- Blood glucose, temperature and weight | These do not detect toxicity.
- Visual acuity, hearing and gait | These are not the monitoring parameters.
= Reflexes disappear before respiratory depression, and falling urine output allows magnesium to accumulate.

? A woman with severe preeclampsia has a sustained BP of 174/116. Which medication should the nurse anticipate?
+ IV labetalol or hydralazine
- An ACE inhibitor | ACE inhibitors are contraindicated in pregnancy.
- A loop diuretic | Diuretics reduce placental perfusion and are not first-line.
- IV nitroprusside as the first choice | It is reserved for refractory cases because of fetal cyanide risk.
= Severe hypertension in pregnancy is treated promptly with IV labetalol or hydralazine or oral nifedipine to prevent stroke.

? A woman at 34 weeks has epigastric pain, nausea and malaise. BP is 146/94, platelets are 68,000/mm3 and liver enzymes are elevated. What should the nurse suspect?
+ HELLP syndrome
- Viral gastroenteritis | That does not cause low platelets and raised liver enzymes.
- Gastric reflux | Reflux does not cause these laboratory changes.
- Appendicitis | That does not cause thrombocytopenia.
= Hemolysis, elevated liver enzymes and low platelets make up HELLP syndrome, which can present with vague abdominal symptoms.

? A woman who delivered a healthy baby five days ago returns with a severe headache and BP 172/108. What should the nurse recognize?
+ Preeclampsia can occur after delivery
- The risk ended with delivery | It persists for up to six weeks postpartum.
- The headache is from lack of sleep | Severe hypertension requires treatment.
- It is a normal postpartum finding | It is a medical emergency.
= Postpartum preeclampsia and eclampsia occur up to six weeks after birth and are treated with magnesium and antihypertensives.

? What is the definitive treatment for preeclampsia?
+ Delivery of the fetus and placenta
- Strict bed rest | Rest does not cure the condition.
- A low-salt diet | Diet does not cure the condition.
- Long-term oral antihypertensives | They control pressure but do not remove the cause.
= The placenta drives the disease; timing of delivery balances maternal risk against fetal maturity.

? A woman at 30 weeks has regular painful contractions and cervical change. Besides stopping contractions, which medication benefits the baby?
+ A corticosteroid such as betamethasone
- An antihistamine | It has no fetal benefit.
- A loop diuretic | It has no fetal benefit.
- An oral iron supplement taken twice daily | It does not affect preterm outcomes.
= Antenatal corticosteroids speed fetal lung maturity and reduce respiratory distress and death in preterm infants.

? During an eclamptic seizure, which nursing action is most appropriate?
+ Turn her onto her left side, protect her from injury and give oxygen
- Hold her limbs still | Restraint causes injury.
- Insert a padded tongue blade between the teeth to protect her tongue from biting | Objects in the mouth cause injury and obstruction.
- Lay her flat on her back | Supine positioning compresses the vena cava and risks aspiration.
= Left lateral positioning protects the airway and uterine blood flow while magnesium is prepared.

@ 8.7
? A woman in labour arrives by car. She says the baby is coming, and the head is visible at the vaginal opening. What should the nurse do?
+ Prepare to deliver the baby where she is
- Hold her legs together until the obstetrician arrives | Delaying delivery can injure the baby.
- Send her to the labour unit in a wheelchair | Birth is imminent and transport is unsafe.
- Ask her to stop pushing and breathe normally for an hour | Crowning means delivery is about to occur.
= Crowning signals imminent birth; the nurse calls for help and supports a controlled delivery.

? The baby's head has delivered and the nurse feels a loop of umbilical cord around the neck. It is loose. What should the nurse do?
+ Slip the cord over the baby's head
- Pull firmly on the cord | Traction can tear the cord or placenta.
- Push the head back in | That is dangerous and ineffective.
- Ask the mother to push hard immediately | The cord should be freed first.
= A loose nuchal cord is eased over the head; a tight one is clamped in two places and cut.

? The head delivers and then pulls back tightly against the perineum, and the shoulders do not follow. Which maneuver should be used first?
+ Flex the mother's thighs sharply onto her abdomen and apply suprapubic pressure
- Apply firm pressure on the top of the uterus while the mother pushes with each pain | Fundal pressure worsens the impaction and can rupture the uterus.
- Pull hard on the baby's head | Traction causes nerve injury.
- Rotate the head forcefully | Twisting the head causes injury.
= Shoulder dystocia is managed first with the McRoberts position and suprapubic pressure.

? A woman's membranes rupture and a loop of umbilical cord is seen at the vagina. What should the nurse do immediately?
+ Lift the presenting part off the cord with a gloved hand and position her knees to chest
- Push the cord gently back into the uterus with two fingers and ask her to keep her legs flat | Handling the cord causes vessel spasm.
- Have her walk to speed delivery | Gravity increases compression of the cord.
- Clamp and cut the cord | That cuts off the fetal blood supply.
= A prolapsed cord is compressed by the presenting part; pressure is relieved by hand and position until emergency delivery.

? A newborn delivered in the ED is limp and not breathing after drying and stimulation. What is the next step?
+ Begin positive pressure ventilation
- Start chest compressions | Compressions come only if the heart rate stays below 60 after effective ventilation.
- Give epinephrine | Medication follows ventilation and compressions.
- Wait and observe for five minutes | Ventilation should start within the first minute.
= Ventilation is the most important step in neonatal resuscitation and should begin within 60 seconds of birth.

? After 30 seconds of effective ventilation, a newborn's heart rate is 48 per minute. What should be done?
+ Start chest compressions coordinated with ventilation at 3 to 1
- Continue ventilation alone for a further five minutes before deciding | A rate under 60 despite ventilation needs compressions.
- Stop resuscitation | Resuscitation is continuing appropriately.
- Give a fluid bolus first | Volume is not the next step.
= A heart rate below 60 after effective ventilation calls for compressions at three compressions to one breath.

? Immediately after delivering a vigorous, crying term baby, what is the priority for the newborn?
+ Drying and keeping the baby warm, ideally skin to skin
- A full bath | Bathing causes heat loss.
- Deep suctioning of the stomach | Routine deep suction is unnecessary and can cause bradycardia.
- Weighing and measuring the baby before anything else is done | Measurement can wait.
= Newborns lose heat rapidly; drying and skin-to-skin contact maintain temperature and support transition.

? Ten minutes after delivery of the placenta a woman is bleeding heavily, and her uterus feels soft and boggy. What should the nurse do first?
+ Massage the fundus firmly
- Insert a urinary catheter and wait | Bladder emptying helps but massage comes first.
- Pack the vagina with gauze | Packing hides ongoing bleeding from an atonic uterus.
- Raise the head of the bed | That does not address the cause.
= Uterine atony is the most common cause of postpartum hemorrhage; fundal massage and oxytocin make the uterus contract.

? A woman with postpartum hemorrhage has a history of chronic hypertension and BP 162/98. Which uterotonic should be avoided?
+ Methylergonovine
- Oxytocin | Oxytocin is first-line and safe in hypertension.
- Fundal massage | It is not a drug and is appropriate.
- Tranexamic acid | It is not contraindicated by hypertension.
= Methylergonovine causes vasoconstriction and can provoke severe hypertension or stroke.

? Four days after giving birth, a woman has a temperature of 38.9 C, lower abdominal pain and foul-smelling vaginal discharge. What should the nurse suspect?
+ Postpartum endometritis
- Normal postpartum bleeding | Normal lochia is not foul smelling and does not cause fever.
- Breast engorgement | That causes breast pain, not uterine tenderness.
- A urinary tract infection only | That does not cause foul lochia.
= Fever, uterine tenderness and foul lochia indicate infection of the uterine lining, treated with IV antibiotics.

@ 8.8
? A woman arrives after a sexual assault with a deep scalp laceration and dizziness. What is the nurse's first priority?
+ Assess and treat her injuries
- Collect forensic evidence | Medical stabilization comes before evidence collection.
- Call the police before anything else | Reporting is her decision and follows care.
- Take a detailed account of the assault | A detailed history can wait until she is stable.
= Life and limb take priority; forensic examination follows once the patient is medically stable.

? A patient who was sexually assaulted two hours ago asks whether she can shower and change before the examination. What is the best response?
+ Explain that waiting preserves evidence, and that the choice is hers
- Tell her she is not permitted to wash | She retains control over what happens.
- Tell her it makes no difference | Washing removes evidence.
- Insist that she showers first so that she is comfortable for the examination | That destroys evidence she may want collected.
= Washing, changing, urinating, eating or drinking can remove evidence; the nurse informs without coercing.

? During a forensic examination the patient says she does not want photographs taken. What should the nurse do?
+ Omit the photographs and continue with the parts she agrees to
- Explain that the whole examination must be completed or none of it | Each part requires her consent.
- Take the photographs while she is distracted | That violates her consent.
- Stop the examination and discharge her | She may still want other care.
= Consent is obtained for each step, and the patient may decline any part without losing access to care.

? Who is best qualified to perform a sexual assault forensic examination?
+ A sexual assault nurse examiner
- The first available medical student | Specific training is required.
- A police officer | Officers do not perform medical examinations.
- A hospital volunteer | Volunteers are not qualified.
= Specially trained examiners provide trauma-informed care, collect evidence correctly and can testify in court.

? A sexual assault patient is undressing for the examination. How should the nurse collect her clothing?
+ Have her stand on a clean paper sheet and place each item in a separate paper bag
- Put all items into one plastic bag | Plastic degrades evidence and items cross-contaminate.
- Shake each item out before bagging | Shaking loses trace evidence.
- Send the clothing home with a friend to be washed and returned to her the following day | That breaks the chain of custody.
= The sheet catches hair and fibres; paper bags allow drying and separate packaging prevents transfer.

? A patient was sexually assaulted 30 hours ago and does not want to become pregnant. What should the nurse offer?
+ Emergency contraception
- A pregnancy test in six weeks only | Prevention is time sensitive.
- Advice to wait for her next period | Waiting forfeits the chance to prevent pregnancy.
- Nothing, as it is too late | Emergency contraception works for up to five days.
= Emergency contraception is offered to all patients at risk of pregnancy, with effectiveness highest when taken early.

? Which preventive treatment is time limited to within 72 hours of a sexual assault?
+ HIV post-exposure prophylaxis
- A tetanus booster | Its timing is not limited to 72 hours.
- An influenza vaccine | It is unrelated to the assault.
- A vitamin supplement | It is not prophylaxis.
= HIV post-exposure prophylaxis must begin within 72 hours; antibiotics for gonorrhea, chlamydia and trichomonas are also offered.

? A 32-year-old sexual assault patient says she does not want the police involved. What should the nurse do?
+ Respect her decision and explain that evidence can still be collected and stored
- Call the police anyway | In most places a capable adult decides whether to report.
- Refuse to provide any further care or evidence collection unless she agrees to report | Care does not depend on reporting.
- Tell her the evidence will be thrown away | Kits can usually be held while she decides.
= Adults generally choose whether to report; anonymous or non-reporting kits preserve evidence. Assault of a child must be reported.

? A patient believes she was drugged at a bar before being assaulted last night. Which specimen is most important to collect promptly?
+ Urine
- Hair from the scalp | Hair testing is useful only weeks later.
- A throat culture | It does not detect sedative drugs.
- A stool sample | It is not used for this purpose.
= Many sedatives are cleared quickly; the first available urine gives the best chance of detection.

? A sexual assault patient says, "I should never have gone to that party." Which response is most therapeutic?
+ "What happened was not your fault."
- "Why did you go alone?" | "Why" questions imply blame.
- "You'll know better next time." | That places responsibility on her.
- "Try not to think about it." | That dismisses her feelings.
= Survivors often blame themselves; a clear, non-judgmental statement that she is not at fault supports recovery.
